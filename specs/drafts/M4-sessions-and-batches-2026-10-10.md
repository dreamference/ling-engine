# M4 design note: resident sessions and batches of two

Draft, 2026-10-10. Part of the ling-engine specification's working notes; the index is [../README.md](../README.md) and `§n` cites the sections it maps. The owner's decision of 2026-10-10: sessions and batches of two are one milestone with one gate, "ready for a benchmark night": two agent tasks served at once at no less than production's two-stream numbers ([reports/M0.md](../../reports/M0.md) §4: 33.9 tokens/s per stream, a 145 ms step period), the benchmark harness admitting two tasks (it divides `sglang:max_total_num_tokens` by 49,152 per task, [API §6](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_API.md)), no restart over 8 hours, and 35K-token prefills at the harness's pace. Of [§14](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_MILESTONES.md)'s original M4 list, images and paged FP8 KV are outside this gate: FP8 KV changes numerics and §14's exactness policy says it waits until the exact engine has beaten SGLang in a night.

## 0. Where the engine stands

`ling-serve` keeps one resident history: `Engine` owns one token history, one flat BF16 KV cache per attention layer, one DeltaNet and conv state per linear layer, the end-of-prompt snapshot `snap_` and up to eight prefix checkpoints (`src/core/engine.hpp`, the "Sequence state" and "Kept states" members; `Engine::prefill` and `Engine::save_checkpoint` in `src/core/engine.cpp`). `class Worker` in `src/serve/main.cpp` runs one job at a time from a queue: `generate()` prefills, then loops `speculate()` or `step()` until the request ends. `Metrics` reports `num_running_reqs` as 0 or 1 and `max_total_num_tokens` as the context size, so the harness admits one task. Every pass of at most 32 rows takes the rows path, whose kernels are row-invariant ([reports/M1.md](../../reports/M1.md) §3): a row's result is bit for bit the same whatever the other rows are. That property is what makes a batch of two exact per sequence, and the whole design leans on it.

## 1. The session store

**What a session is.** The per-sequence members of `Engine` move into a `struct Session`: the token history and `prompt_len_`, the KV caches `kcache_`/`vcache_` (16 attention layers), `gdn_state_`/`conv_state_` (48 linear layers), `snap_` and `snap_pos_`, the drafter's context KV `dkc_`/`dvc_` and `dkv_lo_`, `pos_`, the lookup's `shadow_` records, and `rng_`. The RNG must be per session: `sample()` and `speculate()` reseed from `p.seed + history_.size()`, so a shared generator would make one sequence's seeded draws depend on its partner's, and seeded checks (`--graph-check`'s form) could not hold per sequence. `Engine` keeps what is shared: weights, the drafter, activation scratch, `logits_dev_`, `pin_`, the verify inputs `v_pre_/v_post_/v_g_/v_beta_` (per row, 32 rows, not per session), the graphs, and the checkpoint store. A session is a fixed slot allocated in the constructor (`alloc` is constructor-only today and stays so: nothing is allocated while serving, §8 stage 6), selected by `--sessions N`.

**Memory per session at 65,536 context,** derived from the engine's actual encodings rather than §11's planned ones (§11 and [§5.2](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_MODEL.md) quote 32 KB per token, which is FP8 KV; the engine's caches are BF16 and stay so under §14):

| Part | Arithmetic | Bytes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KV cache, BF16, flat at `max_context` | 16 layers × 2 × 4 heads × 256 × 2 B = 64 KB per token × 65,536 | 4.29 GB |
| DeltaNet state, FP32 | 48 × 48 × 128 × 128 × 4 B | 151 MB |
| Conv window, FP32 | 48 × 10,240 × 3 × 4 B | 5.9 MB |
| `snap_` (the end-of-prompt state) | the two rows above again | 157 MB |
| Drafter context KV, today | 5 layers × 2 × 65,536 × (8 × 128) × 2 B | 1.34 GB |
| Drafter context KV, as a ring | 5 × 2 × (2,048 + 32) × 1,024 × 2 B | 43 MB |
| Token history | 65,536 × 4 B | 0.3 MB |
| **Per session, with the ring** | | **4.65 GB** |

The drafter attends to the last 2,048 positions only ([§10](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_SPECULATION.md); `DraftConfig::sliding_window`), so `dkc_`/`dvc_` become a ring of 2,048 + `kMaxStreamRows` positions indexed by position modulo the ring; `draft_store_kv` and `draft_attention` change their addressing, the data they read is identical, so the change is exact. Two sessions take 9.3 GB, four 18.6 GB; [§12](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_INTEGRATION.md)'s 30 GB session pool holds six at full context. §11's "about 30 sessions in 30 GB" assumes FP8 KV at the workload's 25.7K median; it is not this milestone's number. The shared checkpoint store stays at eight slots of 157 MB (1.26 GB, [reports/M2-prefill.md](../../reports/M2-prefill.md) §4). Scratch that is per engine, not per session: the activation buffers sized for a 2,048-row chunk, `cap_` (2,048 × 5 × 5,120 BF16, 105 MB), `logits_dev_` (32 × 248,320 FP32, 32 MB), the attention partials.

**How a request finds its session.** The prefix decides, as §11 says, because exactness requires the state to be the one after exactly the prompt's tokens; `prompt_cache_key` is where to look first. The scheduler computes, for each resident session, the longest common prefix of the request's token ids with that session's history (what `prefill()`'s `lcp` loop does today against the one history; 65K integer compares per session, microseconds). The request binds to the session with the longest common prefix, provided that prefix reaches past the end of the shared prefix every session carries (the furthest checkpoint position usable, as `prefill()` computes `reuse` today); if no session reaches past it, the request is a new session: it takes a free slot, or evicts one, and resumes from the checkpoint store exactly as a new prompt does today. A session is bound only when the request extends its history (the common prefix is the whole history, or the history past it is that session's own last output, as a next turn's prompt is), never when the two merely overlap: a new task whose prompt shares its first user message with a resident task must not take that task's slot and re-prefill it from the divergence. A request that carries `prompt_cache_key` (Codex sends the thread id, [API §4](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_API.md), [BACKLOG §19.8](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_BACKLOG.md)) is checked against the session holding that key first; if the key's history has diverged from the prompt (a stripped thinking block, an edited system prompt: §11's prefix-stability cases), the prefix rule wins and the session re-prefills from the first divergence, as today. Chat and completions requests have no key; the prefix rule stands alone for them. `Request` in `src/serve/api.hpp` gains `session_key`, filled by `parse_responses_request` from `prompt_cache_key`.

**The checkpoint store owns its identity.** Today a `Checkpoint{pos, slot}` is valid "while those tokens stay in `history_`" and `truncate_history` drops it when the one history is cut. With several sessions a checkpoint must outlive the history it came from: each entry keeps the token prefix it stands for (at most `checkpoint_limit` = 32,768 ids, 128 KB) and a 64-bit hash of it as the prefilter, as §11 says ("keyed by a hash of the token prefix"); a hit is an exact comparison of the ids, never the hash alone. `save_checkpoint`'s rule stays: the earliest positions are the ones sessions share, so the latest gives way when the slots are full. A checkpoint is published only after its copy has completed on the stream ([reports/engine-issues-2026-10-09.md](../../reports/engine-issues-2026-10-09.md), sglang#39342: a mixed schedule must never hand out a slot whose write is pending), and a pass that leaves non-finite logits saves nothing, as today.

**Eviction.** LRU by the time a session's last request ended; a session with a running or queued request is pinned. Eviction frees the slot and nothing is copied out (§11: no separate host memory on this machine). With the slots all pinned a new session's request waits in the queue and `num_queue_reqs` says so; the engine never spins or retries on a full pool (sglang#30314 in the issue survey).

## 2. Batches of two

**The step today** (`src/core/speculate.cpp`): draft a block of B rows from the anchor, walk the selector's lattice on the host, verify the B rows in one `forward(..., Pass::Verify)`, accept on the host from each row's top-K, commit the accepted rows by replaying their saved DeltaNet inputs. A batch of two runs the same four parts for two sequences with the verify's rows concatenated: rows `[a1, d1..]` of sequence 1 then `[a2, d2..]` of sequence 2, V = V1 + V2 ≤ `kMaxStreamRows` = 32. With `ling-serve`'s default `--draft-block 12` that is 24 rows; with 16 it is exactly 32 and no row is left for a longer block.

**What takes a second row unchanged.** The weight-streaming GEMMs (`stream_gemm_nvfp4`, `stream_gemm_fp8`, `stream_gemm_multi`, `bf16_rows`), the norms (`rmsnorm_half`, `rmsnorm`, `gated_rmsnorm`), `silu_mul`, `sigmoid_mul`, `add_inplace`, `embed`, `copy_rows_bf16`, `topk_rows`, `gdn_gating`, and the drafter's projections are per row and row-invariant; `grouped_conv` is block-wise (it takes `block` beside `rows`, the second tap masked at each block's position 0), so over two blocks it runs with `rows = 2B, block = B`; they see 24 or 32 rows and read the weights once. `stream_gemm`'s dispatch moves from the `M ≤ 16` tier to the `M > 16` tier (four activation fragments per warp, `launch<4, ·>`), which changes no element's arithmetic; what the extra rows cost the weight stream is to measure (M1 §4 found the step at 16 rows only 2.8 ms longer than at 12).

**What carries per-sequence state and must learn which rows belong to which sequence** (all in `src/core/kernels.cuh`): `attn_prepare` (writes k/v into one cache at one position), `attention_rows` (one cache, one `pos0`, causal over its rows), `gdn_conv` and `gdn_recurrent` (one window, one state), `draft_qk_rope`, `draft_store_kv` and `draft_attention` (one drafter cache, one position), and `selector_lattice` (one anchor). Two ways to give them the second sequence, both exact because every one of them computes a row from its own sequence's state at fixed positions:

- *Two launches per kernel*, one per sequence over its row range: no kernel changes, only an offset into the row buffers (`q_ + row0 * qsize`, `v_pre_[slot] + row0 * C`) and the session's pointers as arguments. This is the first build.
- *One launch with a sequence index in the grid* (`blockIdx.y` or `.z`) reading a device-side descriptor table `SeqDesc[2]` = {kcache, vcache, gdn state, conv state, drafter ring, pos, row0, rows, anchor}, written before each step the way `pos_dev_` is today (`kernels::DevPos` generalised). For `gdn_recurrent`, one block per head and latency-bound (1.6 ms per verify across 48 layers, [reports/M3.md](../../reports/M3.md) §5), two sequences in one launch cost about what one does; for `attention_rows` the bytes are the KV of both sequences whichever way they are launched.

`forward()` takes a span of `{Session*, row0, rows, pass}` instead of `(ids, M, pass)`; the layer loop launches the row-wise kernels once over all rows and the state-bearing ones per sequence or through the table. `commit(n)` becomes `commit(Session&, row0, n)` and reads its rows from the shared `v_*` buffers at `row0`; the conv-window replay is checked for overlapping copies once rows are offset (vllm#53912 in the survey). `draft_launch(B)` runs the drafter's five layers over both blocks at once (2B rows through the same NVFP4 projections, read once), with `draft_attention` and the RoPE per sequence; the lattice walk, the accept rule and the lookup proposal (`lookup_propose` over the session's own history) run per sequence on the host, unchanged.

**The expected cost, from the step breakdown** (M1 §5's at 31K, as [reports/M3.md](../../reports/M3.md) §5 re-measured it at 34.8K context with graphs on, GPU time per step): NVFP4 and FP8 GEMMs 89.1 ms, attention 11.2, DeltaNet 3.3, the drafter's GEMMs 7.4 and small kernels 3.0, other small kernels ~9, idle 1.1; wall 111 ms at the workload's context.

| Shape | Step per stream | Per-stream decode at 4.9 accepted | Against the gate (33.9 tokens/s, 145 ms) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two sessions, interleaved single-row steps (A, B, A, B) | 2 × 111 = 222 ms period | ~22 tokens/s | fails, by a third |
| Batch of two, state kernels launched twice, drafter per sequence | 89 + rows (to measure) + 2 × 11.2 + 2 × 3.3 + 2 × 10.4 + ~9 + idle ≈ 140–150 ms | ~33–35 | on the line |
| Batch of two, state kernels in one launch, drafter over 2B rows | 89 + rows + 2 × 11.2 + ~3.5 + ~11 + ~9 + idle ≈ 135–145 ms | ~34–36 | on the line |

M0 §9's engine estimate for four streams was ~100 ms per step with FP8 KV and the attention at bandwidth; neither is in this gate. The levers that move the batch step below the line, each exact: the draft block at batch 2 (M0 §6: 12 draft tokens shortened production's two-stream step by 4% at flat acceptance; `ling-serve` already defaults to 12 while `EngineOptions::draft_block` is 16; [BACKLOG B31](../../reports/backlog-2026-10-09.md) is the sweep), the weight stream (B16, ~6 ms), the verify attention's stages (B07, 1–1.5 ms per sequence), and the commit beside the next draft on a second captured stream (M3 §7 item 4, ~2 ms). All of these are "to measure"; the note promises none of them.

**Graphs.** Three things in the captured step are single-sequence: `pos_dev_` is one int, the cache and state pointers are baked kernel arguments, and the draft graph reads its anchor from `ids_dev_[0]`; `commit_graphs_` are keyed by accepted count, which differs per sequence. The batch path therefore runs eagerly until the batch check (section 4 below) passes; then the descriptor table above gives one draft graph and one verify graph per (batch size, top-K) that read everything per sequence from device memory, and commit graphs keyed by (sequence index, count): 2 × 32 at most, captured on first use as today. A failed capture falls back to eager, as `run_graph` does now.

**Speculation per row.** Each sequence keeps its own anchor, block, lattice walk, q-distribution, accept and commit; the only thing they share is the pass. Greedy acceptance of sequence 1 in a batch equals its acceptance alone, because the verify rows' logits are bit-identical (row invariance) and the drafter's block, run over 2B rows, is row-invariant too. A sequence whose context has fewer than B tokens of room, or whose request decodes plainly (penalties set, `can_speculate` false), leaves the batch for that step and runs its own one-row step; the other continues alone. A non-finite row (`require_finite_topk`) fails only its sequence: the verify wrote no state, its commit is skipped, the session is reset, the other sequence commits normally.

## 3. The scheduler

`Worker` becomes `Scheduler` in `src/serve/main.cpp`, still one engine thread (the CPU-idle rule of §16.9 and M0 §1 stands: host work per step stays microseconds). Its loop:

1. **Admission.** While fewer than `--batch` (2) sequences are active and the queue is not empty: take the next job, bind its session (section 1), and make it active. A job whose session is pinned by another active request of the same thread waits (Codex sends one request per thread at a time, so this is the compaction-retry case only). `exceeds_context` is checked per session as today.
2. **Prefill.** An active job that has not finished its prompt runs one prefill chunk through `Engine::prefill_chunk(Session&, PrefillCursor&)`, the body of today's `prefill()` loop with the loop lifted into the scheduler: one `forward(Pass::Prefill)` of up to `prefill_chunk` tokens, checkpoints saved at the same bounds, `snap_` kept at the aligned end. Between chunks, if another sequence is decoding, it gets one step (§11: the chunk size "lets a long prefill be interrupted by a running decode step"). A 2,048-token chunk takes 0.8–1 s at M2's 2,106–2,637 tokens/s, so one step per chunk gives the decoding stream a ~1 s period for the 16 s of a 35K-token prefill; a smaller chunk under contention (512 tokens, ~0.2–0.25 s) bounds that period at about 0.35 s and costs the prefill some throughput that M2 did not measure below 2,048 tokens: `--prefill-chunk-contended N`, default to measure. Two sequences prefilling at once alternate chunks. Running a prefill chunk concurrently with a decode step on a second CUDA stream (compute-bound beside bandwidth-bound) needs a second set of activation buffers (~0.7 GB) and is a measurement after the gate, not part of it.
3. **Decode.** All active sequences past their prompt form the batch; `Engine::speculate_batch(std::span<SeqStep>)` runs one step and returns each sequence's accepted tokens and next token; the scheduler feeds each job's `StreamDecoder`, `StopScanner` and `JobSink` exactly as `generate()` does per token today, including the reasoning-token count. A job that ends (stop, length, cancel, error) leaves the batch; its session stays resident with its LRU time stamped.
4. **Fairness.** Round-robin between prefill and decode as in step 2; within the batch both sequences advance every step by construction. No priorities: two agent tasks are peers.

Counters are per request as today, but the deltas `generate()` takes from the engine-global `SpecStats` (`steps0`, `drafted0`, `accepted0`) break once two requests overlap; `SpecStats` becomes per session and the request reads its own. `Metrics` gauges: `sglang:num_running_reqs` = active sequences (prefilling or decoding, 0–2), `sglang:num_queue_reqs` = waiting jobs, `sglang:max_total_num_tokens` = sessions × `max_context` (2 × 65,536 = 131,072; ⌊131,072 / 49,152⌋ = 2 tasks). Added under the engine's own prefix, per §8's telemetry list: `ling:sessions_resident`, `ling:session_evictions_total`, `ling:batch_rows` (the last step's rows), `ling:prefill_chunks_interleaved_total`, and per-step batch size in `/spec_stats`.

**No restart over 8 hours.** Memory is fixed at construction and graphs are bounded (two draft, a few verify, at most 64 commit); `shadow_` records are scored and dropped as history catches up; histories are bounded by `max_context`; sessions are reused, never reallocated. The soak in [BACKLOG B24](../../reports/backlog-2026-10-09.md)'s form (clocks, power, temperature logged; step time per hour) runs at concurrency 2 and reports device memory and RSS flat.

## 4. What is exact and what is checked

The exactness policy (§14) is unchanged: decode and verify on the rows path, prefill as M2 built it, every number bit-identical to today's one-row path. The batch adds no numeric mode; it adds a claim: **a sequence's output and state in a batch are bit for bit its output and state alone** (the survey's sglang#36548 is the failure to rule out: wrong answers under concurrent DFlash2 load). The checks, all in `tools/ling_run.cpp` and `tests/`:

- `--spec-check`, `--prefix-check`, `--graph-check`: unchanged and still required to pass, with and without `--graphs --pdl`, since the one-row path's kernels are the batch path's kernels.
- `--prefix-check --sessions 2`: the existing sequence of replayed prompts (three turns of one session, two of another, a third session, back to the first) now resumes each prompt from its own session's state, and the states and next tokens must equal a prefill from scratch, as M2 §4 checks for one history. This also checks that the checkpoint store survives the eviction of the history it was taken from.
- `--batch-check` (new): two prompt files decoded as a batch of two, greedy and with seeded sampling, 2,000 tokens, with and without graphs; per sequence the tokens, `state_hash()`, the step count and the accepted drafts must equal the same prompt decoded alone with `--spec` under the same seed. Run also with the two prompts swapped (row order) and with one sequence prefilling while the other decodes (the interleaving in section 3).
- `ling-stream-tests`: `memcmp` of each batched kernel against its per-sequence launch (`gdn_recurrent`, `attention_rows`, `attn_prepare`, `draft_attention` through the descriptor table), and of the drafter ring against the flat cache.
- `ling-spec-tests` and `ling-api-tests`: unchanged; `bench/serve_smoke.py` at concurrency 2.
- The agreement gate (§14): unchanged by construction, since no one-row number moves. One run of `bench/agreement/` against ling-serve at concurrency 2 shows the point production could not make: production's own KL rose to 0.017–0.021 nats at concurrency 4 (M2 §6, batched-prefill numerics); the engine's must be 0 against its concurrency-1 run.
- compute-sanitizer on the batch path, PDL off and on, as M3 §6 did; the debug build on `--batch-check` with short prompts.

## 5. The gate, in M0's form

Measured with M0's harness, unchanged (`bench/replay/replay.py --concurrency 2`, the main set of 12 sessions × 12 requests, production sampling, output capped at the recorded length, server-side counters), two runs per arm in one session with every other engine stopped. Production's two-stream column is M0 §4's: 33.9 tokens/s per stream, 4.90 accepted per step, 144.8 ms per-stream step period, 49.4 output tokens/s end to end over both streams, 5.31 s mean request. M0 §6 puts the two-stream noise band at about ±5%, so the rule is the pooled warm figure over both runs, with neither run below the line by more than the band.

| Line | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Decode per stream, two streams | ≥ 33.9 tokens/s pooled; per-stream step period ≤ 145 ms |
| Aggregate | ≥ 49.4 output tokens/s end to end, both streams, prefill included |
| Single stream, same build | step ≤ 110.5 ms within noise (M3's); the batch path must not slow the one-row path |
| Harness admission | `sglang:max_total_num_tokens` ≥ 98,304 (two sessions resident: 131,072); the harness's own log shows two tasks admitted |
| First requests | TTFT of a session's first request at concurrency 2 reported beside production's at concurrency 2 (M0 measured production's first-request TTFT single-stream only, 8.5–10.1 s; the two-stream reference is measured first); a 35K-token prefill while the other stream decodes: prefill tokens/s and the other stream's step period during it, both reported, the bound being question 3 below |
| Soak | 8 hours at concurrency 2 on the replay in a loop, B24's form: no restart, device memory and RSS flat, step time per hour within thermal drift |
| Exactness | every check in section 4 passes; agreement at concurrency 2 is byte-identical to concurrency 1 |

If the pooled figure lands inside the band of the line, the levers of section 2 are measured one at a time (block 12 first, since it is a flag) and the gate is restated with the one that clears it, as M0 did for tuned SGLang.

## 6. Build order, and what each stage gives the harness

1. **Sessions** (`Session`, the slot pool, prefix binding, the checkpoint store with its own identity, the drafter ring): still one request at a time, but two tasks that alternate no longer evict each other's history (BACKLOG §19.8). The pool gauge reports sessions × context, so the harness admits two tasks; they run one after the other, each warm. Checks: `--prefix-check --sessions 2`; `--spec-check` unchanged.
2. **The scheduler, interleaved** (admission, chunked prefill interruptible by the other sequence's step, two active sequences taking one-row steps in turn, per-session `SpecStats`, the gauges): the two tasks truly run at once. Expected ~22 tokens/s per stream; the harness's two-task night is runnable end to end, below the gate. Nothing numeric changes.
3. **The batched verify** (rows concatenated, state kernels launched per sequence, drafter per sequence, eager): the gate's shape. `--batch-check` lands here. Expected 140–150 ms per step, to measure.
4. **One launch per state kernel and the drafter over 2B rows, through the descriptor table; graphs for the batch path**: the second row of the cost table; `--batch-check` with `--graphs --pdl`.
5. **The gate run**: the replay at concurrency 2, block 12 against 16 at batch 2 (B31), the soak, the agreement run.
6. **After the gate, measured separately**: prefill on a second stream beside decode; paged KV (64-token pages, §8 stage 6) so the pool holds more sessions than slots at the workload's median context; FP8 KV behind its switch under §14's policy; batches of four, which need `kMaxStreamRows` above 32 or a block of 8 per sequence.

Files that change: `src/core/engine.hpp`, `src/core/engine.cpp` (Session, the slot pool, `prefill_chunk`, `forward` over a span of sequences, `commit` with a row offset, the checkpoint store), `src/core/speculate.cpp` (`speculate_batch`, the per-sequence walk and accept, the batched draft), `src/core/kernels.cuh` and `kernels.cu`, `draft_kernels.cu` (the descriptor table, the batched state kernels, the drafter ring), `src/serve/main.cpp` (`Scheduler`, `Metrics`), `src/serve/api.hpp` and `api.cpp` (`session_key`), `tools/ling_run.cpp` (`--batch-check`, `--sessions`), `tests/stream_tests.cu`. `bench/replay/replay.py` already takes `--concurrency`.

## 7. Open questions for the owner

1. **Sessions against batch rows.** Two resident sessions for two batch rows keeps the pool gauge honest and the harness at two tasks. Four resident sessions (18.6 GB) would keep a third and fourth thread warm, but the gauge would then read 262,144 and the harness would admit five tasks against a batch of two, so the gauge would need a rule (sessions × context capped at batch rows × context, or the harness's divisor changed). Which?
2. **The draft block at batch 2.** `ling-serve` defaults to 12 and M1 kept 16 single-stream; 2 × 16 fills the 32 rows. Is the gate run at 12 (M0's two-stream finding) with 16 reported beside it, or the reverse?
3. **"At the harness's pace."** No spec section defines it. Does it mean a per-request timeout the harness enforces on the first request of a task (the 35K prefill), a bound on the other stream's step period during that prefill, or both? The contended chunk size in section 3 is set from the answer.
4. **`prompt_cache_key` as a hint or as a binding.** The note treats it as where to look first, with the token prefix deciding (exactness). The alternative, a key that owns a slot and re-prefills on divergence, changes nothing numerically but changes which session is evicted when a third thread arrives. Confirm the hint.
5. **BF16 flat KV in M4.** §11 and §14 list paged FP8 KV under M4; this note keeps the flat BF16 cache per slot for the gate (exact, 4.29 GB per session) and leaves pages and FP8 to stage 6. Confirm.
6. **Eviction protection.** With two slots and two tasks nothing is evicted; a third client (an interactive user beside the night) would evict the LRU task's history mid-task while its tool runs. Should a session with a request in the last N minutes be protected, so that the third client queues instead?
7. **The production reference.** The gate cites production's two-stream numbers at 16 draft tokens (33.9, 145 ms). Tuned SGLang at 12 measured 34.2 and 142.8 ms, inside the noise. Is the line production's, as decided, or the tuned one?
8. **The one-row regression bound.** The descriptor indirection and the row offsets touch the one-row path. Is "within noise of M3's 110.5 ms" the right bound, or must the single-stream step be byte-identical in launch sequence too (no indirection when the batch is one)?
