# M4 design note: resident sessions and batches of two

Draft, 2026-10-10. Part of the ling-engine specification's working notes; the index is [../README.md](../README.md) and `§n` cites the sections it maps. The owner's decision of 2026-10-10: sessions and batches of two are one milestone with one gate, "ready for a benchmark night": two agent tasks served at once at no less than production's two-stream numbers ([reports/M0.md](../../reports/M0.md) §4: 33.9 tokens/s per stream, a 145 ms step period), the benchmark harness admitting two tasks (it divides `sglang:max_total_num_tokens` by 49,152 per task, [API §6](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_API.md)), no restart over 8 hours, and 35K-token prefills at the harness's pace. Of [§14](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_MILESTONES.md)'s original M4 list, images and paged FP8 KV are outside this gate: FP8 KV changes numerics and §14's exactness policy says it waits until the exact engine has beaten SGLang in a night. Images return to M4 after the gate run, with structured output beside them (the owner's decision of 2026-10-12, section 6).

## 0. Where the engine stands

`ling-serve` keeps one resident history: `Engine` owns one token history, one flat BF16 KV cache per attention layer, one DeltaNet and conv state per linear layer, the end-of-prompt snapshot `snap_` and up to eight prefix checkpoints (`src/core/engine.hpp`, the "Sequence state" and "Kept states" members; `Engine::prefill` and `Engine::save_checkpoint` in `src/core/engine.cpp`). `class Worker` in `src/serve/main.cpp` runs one job at a time from a queue: `generate()` prefills, then loops `speculate()` or `step()` until the request ends. `Metrics` reports `num_running_reqs` as 0 or 1 and `max_total_num_tokens` as the context size, so the harness admits one task. Every pass of at most 32 rows takes the rows path, whose kernels are row-invariant ([reports/M1.md](../../reports/M1.md) §3): a row's result is bit for bit the same whatever the other rows are. That property is what makes a batch of two exact per sequence, and the whole design leans on it.

## 1. The session store

**What a session is.** The per-sequence members of `Engine` move into a `struct Session`: the token history and `prompt_len_`, the KV caches `kcache_`/`vcache_` (16 attention layers), `gdn_state_`/`conv_state_` (48 linear layers), `snap_` and `snap_pos_`, the drafter's context KV `dkc_`/`dvc_` and `dkv_lo_`, `pos_`, the lookup's `shadow_` records, and `rng_`. The RNG must be per session: `sample()` and `speculate()` reseed from `p.seed + history_.size()`, so a shared generator would make one sequence's seeded draws depend on its partner's, and seeded checks (`--graph-check`'s form) could not hold per sequence. `Engine` keeps what is shared: weights, the drafter, activation scratch, `logits_dev_`, `pin_`, the verify inputs `v_pre_/v_post_/v_g_/v_beta_` (per row, 32 rows, not per session), the graphs, and the checkpoint store. A session is a fixed slot allocated in the constructor (`alloc` is constructor-only today and stays so: nothing is allocated while serving, §8 stage 6), selected by `--sessions N`.

**Memory per session at 65,536 context,** derived from the engine's actual encodings rather than §11's planned ones (§11 and [§5.2](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_MODEL.md) quote 32 KB per token, which is FP8 KV; the engine's caches are BF16 and stay so under §14):

| Part | Arithmetic | Bytes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KV cache, BF16, flat at `max_context` | 16 layers × 2 × 4 heads × 256 × 2 B = 64 KB per token × 65,536 | 4.29 GB |
| DeltaNet state, FP32 | 48 × 48 × 128 × 128 × 4 B | 151 MB |
| Conv window, FP32 | 48 × 10,240 × 3 × 4 B | 5.9 MB |
| `snap_` (the end-of-prompt state) | the two rows above again | 157 MB |
| Drafter context KV, today | 5 layers × 2 × 65,536 × (8 × 128) × 2 B | 1.34 GB |
| Drafter context KV, as a ring | 5 × 2 × (2,048 + 32) × 1,024 × 2 B | 43 MB |
| Drafter context KV, the ring as built (stage 1: 2,048 + 6,144 slack + 32 positions) | 5 × 2 × 8,224 × 1,024 × 2 B | 168 MB |
| Token history | 65,536 × 4 B | 0.3 MB |
| **Per session, with the ring** | | **4.65 GB** |

The drafter attends to the last 2,048 positions only ([§10](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_SPECULATION.md); `DraftConfig::sliding_window`), so `dkc_`/`dvc_` become a ring of 2,048 + `kMaxStreamRows` positions indexed by position modulo the ring; `draft_store_kv` and `draft_attention` change their addressing, the data they read is identical, so the change is exact. Two sessions take 9.3 GB, four 18.6 GB; [§12](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_INTEGRATION.md)'s 30 GB session pool holds six at full context. §11's "about 30 sessions in 30 GB" assumes FP8 KV at the workload's 25.7K median; it is not this milestone's number. The shared checkpoint store stays at eight slots of 157 MB (1.26 GB, [reports/M2-prefill.md](../../reports/M2-prefill.md) §4). Scratch that is per engine, not per session: the activation buffers sized for a 2,048-row chunk, `cap_` (2,048 × 5 × 5,120 BF16, 105 MB), `logits_dev_` (32 × 248,320 FP32, 32 MB), the attention partials.

**How a request finds its session.** The prefix decides, as §11 says, because exactness requires the state to be the one after exactly the prompt's tokens; `prompt_cache_key` is where to look first. The scheduler computes, for each resident session, the longest common prefix of the request's token ids with that session's history (what `prefill()`'s `lcp` loop does today against the one history; 65K integer compares per session, microseconds). The request binds to the session with the longest common prefix, provided that prefix reaches past the end of the shared prefix every session carries (the furthest checkpoint position usable, as `prefill()` computes `reuse` today); if no session reaches past it, the request is a new session: it takes a free slot, or evicts one, and resumes from the checkpoint store exactly as a new prompt does today. A session is bound only when the request extends its history (the common prefix is the whole history, or the history past it is that session's own last output, as a next turn's prompt is), never when the two merely overlap: a new task whose prompt shares its first user message with a resident task must not take that task's slot and re-prefill it from the divergence. A request that carries `prompt_cache_key` (Codex sends the thread id, [API §4](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_API.md), [BACKLOG §19.8](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_BACKLOG.md)) is checked against the session holding that key first; if the key's history has diverged from the prompt (a stripped thinking block, an edited system prompt: §11's prefix-stability cases), the prefix rule wins and the session re-prefills from the first divergence, as today. Chat and completions requests have no key; the prefix rule stands alone for them. `Request` in `src/serve/api.hpp` gains `session_key`, filled by `parse_responses_request` from `prompt_cache_key`.

**The checkpoint store owns its identity.** Today a `Checkpoint{pos, slot}` is valid "while those tokens stay in `history_`" and `truncate_history` drops it when the one history is cut. With several sessions a checkpoint must outlive the history it came from: each entry keeps the token prefix it stands for (at most `checkpoint_limit` = 32,768 ids, 128 KB) and a 64-bit hash of it as the prefilter, as §11 says ("keyed by a hash of the token prefix"); a hit is an exact comparison of the ids, never the hash alone. `save_checkpoint`'s rule stays: the earliest positions are the ones sessions share, so the latest gives way when the slots are full. **Changed (the owner, 2026-10-12):** the least recently used entry gives way, so a new prompt's checkpoints are saved however full the store is; an entry is used when it is saved and when a prompt's prefill resumes from it or extends its prefix, and a prefill never evicts an entry on its own prompt's prefix, so a prompt longer than the store still keeps its earliest positions ("Stage 3 as built"). A checkpoint is published only after its copy has completed on the stream ([reports/engine-issues-2026-10-09.md](../../reports/engine-issues-2026-10-09.md), sglang#39342: a mixed schedule must never hand out a slot whose write is pending), and a pass that leaves non-finite logits saves nothing, as today.

**Eviction.** LRU by the time a session's last request ended; a session with a running or queued request is pinned. Eviction frees the slot and nothing is copied out (§11: no separate host memory on this machine). With the slots all pinned a new session's request waits in the queue and `num_queue_reqs` says so; the engine never spins or retries on a full pool (sglang#30314 in the issue survey).

## 2. Batches of two

**The step today** (`src/core/speculate.cpp`): draft a block of B rows from the anchor, walk the selector's lattice on the host, verify the B rows in one `forward(..., Pass::Verify)`, accept on the host from each row's top-K, commit the accepted rows by replaying their saved DeltaNet inputs. A batch of two runs the same four parts for two sequences with the verify's rows concatenated: rows `[a1, d1..]` of sequence 1 then `[a2, d2..]` of sequence 2, V = V1 + V2 ≤ `kMaxStreamRows` = 32. With `ling-serve`'s default `--draft-block 12` that is 24 rows; with 16 it is exactly 32 and no row is left for a longer block.

**What takes a second row unchanged.** The weight-streaming GEMMs (`stream_gemm_nvfp4`, `stream_gemm_fp8`, `stream_gemm_multi`, `bf16_rows`), the norms (`rmsnorm_half`, `rmsnorm`, `gated_rmsnorm`), `silu_mul`, `sigmoid_mul`, `add_inplace`, `embed`, `copy_rows_bf16`, `topk_rows`, `gdn_gating`, and the drafter's projections are per row and row-invariant; `grouped_conv` is block-wise (it takes `block` beside `rows`, the second tap masked at each block's position 0), so over two blocks it runs with `rows = 2B, block = B`; they see 24 or 32 rows and read the weights once. `stream_gemm`'s dispatch moves from the `M ≤ 16` tier to the `M > 16` tier (four activation fragments per warp, `launch<4, ·>`), which changes no element's arithmetic; what the extra rows cost the weight stream is to measure (M1 §4 found the step at 16 rows only 2.8 ms longer than at 12).

**What carries per-sequence state and must learn which rows belong to which sequence** (all in `src/core/kernels.cuh`): `attn_prepare` (writes k/v into one cache at one position), `attention_rows` (one cache, one `pos0`, causal over its rows), `gdn_conv` and `gdn_recurrent` (one window, one state), `draft_qk_rope`, `draft_store_kv` and `draft_attention` (one drafter cache, one position), and `selector_lattice` (one anchor). Two ways to give them the second sequence, both exact because every one of them computes a row from its own sequence's state at fixed positions:

- *Two launches per kernel*, one per sequence over its row range: no kernel changes, only an offset into the row buffers (`q_ + row0 * qsize`, `v_pre_[slot] + row0 * C`) and the session's pointers as arguments. This is the first build.
- *One launch with a sequence index in the grid* (`blockIdx.y` or `.z`) reading a device-side descriptor table `SeqDesc[2]` = {kcache, vcache, gdn state, conv state, drafter ring, pos, row0, rows, anchor}, written before each step the way `pos_dev_` is today (`kernels::DevPos` generalised). For `gdn_recurrent`, one block per head and latency-bound (1.6 ms per verify across 48 layers, [reports/M3.md](../../reports/M3.md) §5), two sequences in one launch cost about what one does; for `attention_rows` the bytes are the KV of both sequences whichever way they are launched.

`forward()` takes a span of `{Session*, row0, rows, pass}` instead of `(ids, M, pass)`; the layer loop launches the row-wise kernels once over all rows and the state-bearing ones per sequence or through the table. `commit(n)` becomes `commit(Session&, row0, n)` and reads its rows from the shared `v_*` buffers at `row0`; the conv-window replay is checked for overlapping copies once rows are offset (vllm#53912 in the survey). `draft_launch(B)` runs the drafter's five layers over both blocks at once (2B rows through the same NVFP4 projections, read once), with `draft_attention` and the RoPE per sequence; the lattice walk, the accept rule and the lookup proposal (`lookup_propose` over the session's own history) run per sequence on the host, unchanged.

**The expected cost, from the step breakdown** (M1 §5's at 31K, as [reports/M3.md](../../reports/M3.md) §5 re-measured it at 34.8K context with graphs on, GPU time per step): NVFP4 and FP8 GEMMs 89.1 ms, attention 11.2, DeltaNet 3.3, the drafter's GEMMs 7.4 and small kernels 3.0, other small kernels ~9, idle 1.1; wall 111 ms at the workload's context.

| Shape | Step per stream | Per-stream decode at 4.9 accepted | Against the gate (33.9 tokens/s, 145 ms) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two sessions, interleaved single-row steps (A, B, A, B) | 2 × 111 = 222 ms period | ~22 tokens/s | fails, by a third |
| Batch of two, state kernels launched twice, drafter per sequence | 89 + rows (to measure) + 2 × 11.2 + 2 × 3.3 + 2 × 10.4 + ~9 + idle ≈ 140–150 ms | ~33–35 | on the line |
| *Measured, stage 4 (one launch, drafter over 2B rows, graphs): the batched step* | draft 13.3 + verify and accept 109.9 + commit 7.1 ≈ 130 ms | 32.2–34.8 on the replay (pooled 33.4), at a 152 ms per-stream period with the prefill interference | the step is 15 ms under the line; the per-stream period is 7 ms over it |
| *Measured, stage 3 (eager): the batched step* | draft 20.4 + verify and accept 110 + commit 7.0 ≈ 137 ms (against 102 ms for one sequence's step the same day) | 30.7–31.2 on the replay, with the prefill interference below | the step is under the line; the per-stream period (157 ms) is not |
| Batch of two, state kernels in one launch, drafter over 2B rows | 89 + rows + 2 × 11.2 + ~3.5 + ~11 + ~9 + idle ≈ 135–145 ms | ~34–36 | on the line |

M0 §9's engine estimate for four streams was ~100 ms per step with FP8 KV and the attention at bandwidth; neither is in this gate. The levers that move the batch step below the line, each exact: the draft block at batch 2 (M0 §6: 12 draft tokens shortened production's two-stream step by 4% at flat acceptance; `ling-serve` already defaults to 12 while `EngineOptions::draft_block` is 16; [BACKLOG B31](../../reports/backlog-2026-10-09.md) is the sweep), the weight stream (B16, ~6 ms), the verify attention's stages (B07, 1–1.5 ms per sequence), and the commit beside the next draft on a second captured stream (M3 §7 item 4, ~2 ms). All of these are "to measure"; the note promises none of them.

**Graphs.** Three things in the captured step are single-sequence: `pos_dev_` is one int, the cache and state pointers are baked kernel arguments, and the draft graph reads its anchor from `ids_dev_[0]`; `commit_graphs_` are keyed by accepted count, which differs per sequence. The batch path therefore runs eagerly until the batch check (section 4 below) passes; then the descriptor table above gives one draft graph and one verify graph per (batch size, top-K) that read everything per sequence from device memory, and commit graphs keyed by (sequence index, count): 2 × 32 at most, captured on first use as today. A failed capture falls back to eager, as `run_graph` does now.

**Speculation per row.** Each sequence keeps its own anchor, block, lattice walk, q-distribution, accept and commit; the only thing they share is the pass. Greedy acceptance of sequence 1 in a batch equals its acceptance alone, because the verify rows' logits are bit-identical (row invariance) and the drafter's block, run over 2B rows, is row-invariant too. A sequence whose context has fewer than B tokens of room, or whose request decodes plainly (penalties set, `can_speculate` false), leaves the batch for that step and runs its own one-row step; the other continues alone. A non-finite row (`require_finite_topk`) fails only its sequence: the verify wrote no state, its commit is skipped, the session is reset, the other sequence commits normally.

## 3. The scheduler

`Worker` becomes `Scheduler` in `src/serve/main.cpp`, still one engine thread (the CPU-idle rule of §16.9 and M0 §1 stands: host work per step stays microseconds). Its loop:

1. **Admission.** While fewer than `--batch` (2) sequences are active and the queue is not empty: take the next job, bind its session (section 1), and make it active. A job whose session is pinned by another active request of the same thread waits (Codex sends one request per thread at a time, so this is the compaction-retry case only). `exceeds_context` is checked per session as today.
2. **Prefill.** An active job that has not finished its prompt runs one prefill chunk through `Engine::prefill_chunk(Session&, PrefillCursor&)`, the body of today's `prefill()` loop with the loop lifted into the scheduler: one `forward(Pass::Prefill)` of up to `prefill_chunk` tokens, checkpoints saved at the same bounds, `snap_` kept at the aligned end. Between chunks, if another sequence is decoding, it gets one step (§11: the chunk size "lets a long prefill be interrupted by a running decode step"). A 2,048-token chunk takes 0.8–1 s at M2's 2,106–2,637 tokens/s, so one step per chunk gives the decoding stream a ~1 s period for the 16 s of a 35K-token prefill; a smaller chunk under contention (512 tokens, ~0.2–0.25 s) bounds that period at about 0.35 s and costs the prefill some throughput that M2 did not measure below 2,048 tokens: `--prefill-chunk-contended N`, default to measure. Two sequences prefilling at once alternate chunks. Running a prefill chunk concurrently with a decode step on a second CUDA stream (compute-bound beside bandwidth-bound) needs a second set of activation buffers (~0.7 GB) and is a measurement after the gate, not part of it.
3. **Decode.** All active sequences past their prompt form the batch; `Engine::speculate_batch(std::span<SeqStep>)` runs one step and returns each sequence's accepted tokens and next token; the scheduler feeds each job's `StreamDecoder`, `StopScanner` and `JobSink` exactly as `generate()` does per token today, including the reasoning-token count. A job that ends (stop, length, cancel, error) leaves the batch; its session stays resident with its LRU time stamped.
4. **Fairness.** Round-robin between prefill and decode as in step 2; within the batch both sequences advance every step by construction. No priorities: two agent tasks are peers.

Counters are per request as today, but the deltas `generate()` takes from the engine-global `SpecStats` (`steps0`, `drafted0`, `accepted0`) break once two requests overlap; `SpecStats` becomes per session and the request reads its own. `Metrics` gauges: `sglang:num_running_reqs` = active sequences (prefilling or decoding, 0–2), `sglang:num_queue_reqs` = waiting jobs, `sglang:max_total_num_tokens` = sessions × `max_context` (2 × 65,536 = 131,072; ⌊131,072 / 49,152⌋ = 2 tasks). Added under the engine's own prefix, per §8's telemetry list: `ling:sessions_resident`, `ling:session_evictions_total`, `ling:batch_rows` (the last step's rows), `ling:prefill_chunks_interleaved_total`, and per-step batch size in `/spec_stats`.

**No restart over 8 hours.** Memory is fixed at construction and graphs are bounded (two draft, a few verify, at most 64 commit); `shadow_` records are scored and dropped as history catches up; histories are bounded by `max_context`; sessions are reused, never reallocated. The soak in [BACKLOG B24](../../reports/backlog-2026-10-09.md)'s form (clocks, power, temperature logged; step time per hour) runs at concurrency 2 and reports device memory and RSS flat.

## 4. What is exact and what is checked

The exactness policy (§14) is unchanged: decode and verify on the rows path, prefill as M2 built it, every number bit-identical to today's one-row path. The batch adds no numeric mode; it adds a claim: **a sequence's output and state in a batch are bit for bit its output and state alone** (the survey's sglang#36548 is the failure to rule out: wrong answers under concurrent DFlash2 load). The checks, all in `tools/ling_run.cpp` and `tests/`:

- `--spec-check`, `--prefix-check`, `--graph-check`: unchanged and still required to pass, with and without `--graphs --pdl`, since the one-row path's kernels are the batch path's kernels.
- `--prefix-check --sessions 2`: the existing sequence of replayed prompts (three turns of one session, two of another, a third session, back to the first) now resumes each prompt from its own session's state, and the states and next tokens must equal a prefill from scratch, as M2 §4 checks for one history. This also checks that the checkpoint store survives the eviction of the history it was taken from.
- `--chunk-check N` and `--interleave-check` (stage 2, built 2026-10-12): a prompt prefilled in chunks of N tokens leaves the state, next token and speculative continuation (tokens, state, steps, accepted drafts) it leaves in full chunks; and two sequences interleaved as the stage-2 scheduler runs them (A decoding while B prefills in contended chunks between A's steps, then steps in turn) give per sequence the tokens, state, steps and accepted drafts of each alone, greedy and seeded, both orders, with and without `--graphs --pdl`.
- `--batch-check` (new): two prompt files decoded as a batch of two, greedy and with seeded sampling, 2,000 tokens, with and without graphs; per sequence the tokens, `state_hash()`, the step count and the accepted drafts must equal the same prompt decoded alone with `--spec` under the same seed. Run also with the two prompts swapped (row order) and with one sequence prefilling while the other decodes (the interleaving in section 3).
- `ling-stream-tests`: `memcmp` of each batched kernel against its per-sequence launch (`gdn_recurrent`, `attention_rows`, `attn_prepare`, `draft_attention` through the descriptor table), and of the drafter ring against the flat cache.
- `ling-spec-tests` and `ling-api-tests`: unchanged; `bench/serve_smoke.py` at concurrency 2.
- The agreement gate (§14): unchanged by construction, since no one-row number moves. One run of `bench/agreement/` against ling-serve at concurrency 2 shows the point production could not make: production's own KL rose to 0.017–0.021 nats at concurrency 4 (M2 §6, batched-prefill numerics); the engine's must be 0 against its concurrency-1 run.
- compute-sanitizer on the batch path, PDL off and on, as M3 §6 did; the debug build on `--batch-check` with short prompts.

## 5. The gate, in M0's form

Measured with M0's harness, unchanged (`bench/replay/replay.py --concurrency 2`, the main set of 12 sessions × 12 requests, production sampling, output capped at the recorded length, server-side counters), two runs per arm in one session with every other engine stopped. Production's two-stream column is M0 §4's: 33.9 tokens/s per stream, 4.90 accepted per step, 144.8 ms per-stream step period, 49.4 output tokens/s end to end over both streams, 5.31 s mean request. M0 §6 puts the two-stream noise band at about ±5%, so the rule is the pooled warm figure over both runs, with neither run below the line by more than the band.

| Line | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Decode per stream, two streams | ≥ 33.9 tokens/s pooled; per-stream step period ≤ 145 ms |
| Aggregate | ≥ 49.4 output tokens/s end to end, both streams, prefill included |
| Single stream, same build | step ≤ 110.5 ms within noise (M3's); the batch path must not slow the one-row path |
| Harness admission | `sglang:max_total_num_tokens` ≥ 98,304 (two sessions resident: 131,072); the harness's own log shows two tasks admitted |
| First requests | TTFT of a session's first request at concurrency 2 reported beside production's at concurrency 2 (M0 measured production's first-request TTFT single-stream only, 8.5–10.1 s; the two-stream reference is measured first); a 35K-token prefill while the other stream decodes: prefill tokens/s and the other stream's step period during it, both reported, the bound being question 3 below |
| Soak | 8 hours at concurrency 2 on the replay in a loop, B24's form: no restart, device memory and RSS flat, step time per hour within thermal drift |
| Exactness | every check in section 4 passes; agreement at concurrency 2 is byte-identical to concurrency 1 |

If the pooled figure lands inside the band of the line, the levers of section 2 are measured one at a time (block 12 first, since it is a flag) and the gate is restated with the one that clears it, as M0 did for tuned SGLang.

## 6. Build order, and what each stage gives the harness

1. **Sessions** (`Session`, the slot pool, prefix binding, the checkpoint store with its own identity, the drafter ring): still one request at a time, but two tasks that alternate no longer evict each other's history (BACKLOG §19.8). The pool gauge reports sessions × context, so the harness admits two tasks; they run one after the other, each warm. Checks: `--prefix-check --sessions 2`; `--spec-check` unchanged. **Done 2026-10-11** (branch `m4/sessions`; as built and measured: "Stage 1 as built" below).
2. **The scheduler, interleaved** (admission, chunked prefill interruptible by the other sequence's step, two active sequences taking one-row steps in turn, per-session `SpecStats`, the gauges): the two tasks truly run at once. Expected ~22 tokens/s per stream; the harness's two-task night is runnable end to end, below the gate. Nothing numeric changes. **Built 2026-10-12 on branch `m4/stage2`, merged into main 2026-10-12 (`17b292b`)**: 22.1 tokens/s per stream at a 219 ms per-stream step period on the replay at concurrency 2, as expected; every exactness check passes; the contended prefill chunk cannot hold the other stream's step to about twice its normal step (question 3), see "Stage 2 as built" below.
3. **The batched verify** (rows concatenated, state kernels launched per sequence, drafter per sequence, eager): the gate's shape. `--batch-check` lands here. Expected 140–150 ms per step, to measure. Also here (the owner's decision, 2026-10-12): the checkpoint store's policy when full becomes LRU eviction, the least recently used entry giving way, in place of stage 1's "the latest position gives way" (the case stage 2's server run showed, "Stage 2 as built"). **Built 2026-10-12 on branch `m4/stage3`, merged into main 2026-10-13**: the batched step measures about 137 ms; at two streams on the replay 30.7 and 31.2 tokens/s per stream at a 157 ms per-stream step period (main the same day: 23.5 and 216.5 ms), below the gate's 33.9 and 145 because 13% of the steps run beside the other request's prefill (265 ms each); the steps with no prefill between them run at 137 ms. Every exactness check passes, with the new `--batch-check`; see "Stage 3 as built" below.
4. **One launch per state kernel and the drafter over 2B rows, through the descriptor table; graphs for the batch path**: the second row of the cost table; `--batch-check` with `--graphs --pdl`. **Built 2026-10-13 on branch `m4/stage4`, merged into main 2026-10-13 (the owner's decision)**: the batched step measures about 130 ms (stage 3: 137); at two streams on the replay 34.8 and 32.2 tokens/s per stream at a 149.6 and 153.8 ms per-stream step period (pooled over both runs 33.4 tokens/s and 151.9 ms, against the gate's 33.9 and 145); every exactness check passes, with `--batch-check` under `--graphs --pdl` on the batch path's own graphs; see "Stage 4 as built" below.
5. **The gate run**: the replay at concurrency 2, block 12 against 16 at batch 2 (B31, measured first in the step savings below), the soak, the agreement run, with the kept step savings and the moonshot's P1 in (P1's GPU tests and first numbers run just before it: the owner's decision of 2026-10-13).
6. **After the gate, measured separately**: prefill on a second stream beside decode (**done 2026-10-17**: `ling-serve --side-prefill 40` by default, [M4-prefill-beside-decode](./M4-prefill-beside-decode-2026-10-17.md); the two-stream aggregate 52.2-52.3 tokens/s against production's 53.1 and 50.6, 36.5-36.6 per stream, the other stream at 205 ms per step during a 35K prefill against SGLang's 2.1 s), bundled with cuBLAS removed (done and merged 2026-10-16) (the owner's decision of 2026-10-14, [BACKLOG §19.23](../DREAMFERENCE_LING_ENGINE_BACKLOG.md): the large-M BF16 GEMM of the prefill as the engine's own tiled kernel, no slower and exact, then `cublas`/`cublasLt` out of the link), the item right after stage 5; paged KV (64-token pages, §8 stage 6) so the pool holds more sessions than slots at the workload's median context; FP8 KV behind its switch under §14's policy; batches of four, which need `kMaxStreamRows` above 32 or a block of 8 per sequence.

### Stage 1 as built (2026-10-11)

`Engine` (`src/core/engine.hpp`) holds `EngineOptions::sessions` slots of `struct Session`, all allocated in the constructor: the KV caches, DeltaNet and conv state, `snap`, the drafter's context ring, the history, `prompt_len`, the RNG, the lookup's shadow records, and the step graphs, which are per session because a captured graph bakes the session's cache and state pointers into its kernels' arguments (each session captures its own on first use). `ling-serve --sessions N` (default 2) and `ling-run --sessions N` select the count; `ling-serve --session-protect-seconds S` (default 30; 180 as first built, 30 by the owner's decision of 2026-10-11) sets the protection window. Requests still run one at a time. What the build settled that section 1 left open:

- **Binding.** A request binds to a resident session when its common prefix with the session's history reaches the start of that session's last turn (the position after the last `<|im_end|>` of its last prompt; a raw prompt without one must be extended whole). That is section 1's "extends" rule with the generation prompt counted as part of the turn: a chat template renders the previous turn's `<|im_start|>assistant` header differently once it is history (no empty think block), so "the common prefix covers the whole last prompt" would never hold for a multi-turn chat. Among the sessions extended, the longest common prefix wins, the key's session on a tie; when none is extended, the key's own session takes the request (the same thread, diverged: it re-prefills from the divergence); otherwise the request is a new session. `prompt_cache_key` is read on `/v1/responses` and, harmlessly, on the chat and completions routes.
- **Eviction and protection.** A new session takes a never-used slot, else the least recently used one that is neither pinned (a queued request continues it) nor protected. Protected: a session bound with a key whose last request ran less than the window ago (question 6). Sessions without a key (chat UIs, the replay, the server suite) are protected only while a request of theirs runs or waits: applied to them, the window made every third unrelated short request wait out the window (the suite's 60 short requests in a row). When a new session's request cannot be placed it waits in the queue, `num_queue_reqs` counts it, and the worker re-checks once a second while requests behind it that continue a resident session run; a waiting request whose client leaves is dropped when the server next writes to it (the 15 s keep-alive). Eviction copies nothing and clears nothing: the slot's tokens stay usable as KV by any later prompt that shares them, as the one history was before.
- **Reuse across sessions.** The prompt resumes from the furthest kept state (a session's `snap` or a store entry whose token prefix equals the prompt's) that lies inside the common prefix of the prompt with *some* session's history. When that session is not the active one, its KV for the prefix (`cudaMemcpy2DAsync`, head-major rows) and its drafter ring are copied in first; copies are exact, so a new task's first request still starts after the shared system prompt (`--prefix-check --sessions 2`'s prompt 3 resumes at 11,488 in an empty slot from session 0's KV).
- **The checkpoint store** keeps, per entry, the token prefix (at most 32,768 ids) and its FNV-1a hash; a hit is the hash and then the ids. `truncate_history` no longer touches it; `save_checkpoint` deduplicates by position and prefix and keeps its rule (the latest position gives way when full; least recently used since 2026-10-12, see section 1 and "Stage 3 as built"). One stream orders the copy before any restore.
- **The drafter ring.** Position p lives in row p % ring of each drafter layer's context KV (`draft_store_kv`, `draft_attention`; the data read is identical, so the change is exact). The ring holds 2,048 + 6,144 + 32 = 8,224 positions (168 MB per session, not section 1's 43 MB): a resume at p > L − 2,048 needs the ring to still hold [L − 2,048, p), which fails once the session's history ran more than ring − 2,048 positions past the new prompt, the case of a re-rendered last turn (a stripped thinking block of up to ~6K tokens). The valid range is [max(`dkv_lo`, history − ring), history), kept across truncations.
- **Memory**: 4.77 GB per session at 65,536 (the table above with the built ring); two sessions 9.5 GB against one history's 5.9 GB before.
- **Gauges**: `sglang:max_total_num_tokens` = sessions × `max_context` = 131,072 (⌊131,072 / 49,152⌋ = 2 tasks); new `ling:sessions`, `ling:sessions_resident`, `ling:session_evictions_total`, `ling:session_protect_seconds`.

### Stage 2 as built (2026-10-12)

`Worker` in `src/serve/main.cpp` became `Scheduler`, still one engine thread. Up to `ling-serve --batch N` (default 2, at most `--sessions`) requests run at once, each in its own resident session; the running requests take turns, one unit each: a prefill chunk while the prompt is not done, else one decode step (speculative or plain), with the session made active before each unit (`Engine::select_session`). The prefill became a cursor (`Engine::begin_prefill`, which chooses the state to resume from and readies the session, and `Engine::prefill_chunk(cursor, max_tokens)`, one pass with the snap and checkpoint bookkeeping of before); `Engine::prefill` is the loop over them, so `ling-run` and the checks are unchanged. While another request decodes, a prefill runs in chunks of `--prefill-chunk-contended` tokens (default 256), rounded down to a multiple of 32 so every chunk end is a position a resumed prefill could start from; without a decoding partner it runs in full chunks of 2,048. What the build settled that section 3 left open:

- **Admission.** A queued request is admitted while fewer than `--batch` run: bound to the session it continues unless a running request holds that session (it waits: one request per session at a time, the compaction-retry case), else to a new session in a slot no running or queued request holds (stage 1's protection unchanged, the window 30 s since 2026-10-11). A job that cannot be placed is looked at again when the queue or the running set changes, or once a second.
- **Counters.** `SpecStats` is per session; a request's deltas are its own session's; `/spec_stats` is the sum over the sessions (`Engine::spec_stats_total`), so `bench/replay/spec_report.py` reads it as before. New: `ling:batch`, `ling:prefill_chunk_contended`, `ling:prefill_chunks_interleaved_total`; `sglang:num_running_reqs` counts running requests (0 to `--batch`). The request log line adds the prefill's chunks (and how many ran beside a decode), its compute rate, and the request's step periods (start to start), apart from those with another request's prefill chunk in between.

### Stage 3 as built (2026-10-12)

`Engine::speculate_batch(std::span<SeqStep>)` (`src/core/speculate.cpp`) runs one speculative step for each of several sessions. `forward` became `forward_rows` over a span of `Seg{Session*, row0, rows}` (`forward` is one segment, the active session's): the row-wise kernels run once over all V rows, and the state-bearing ones per segment: `attn_prepare` and `attention_rows` over the sequence's rows, cache and position, the verify's conv on a copy of the sequence's own window, the recurrence from its own state. Each sequence's top K is taken over its own rows with its own K (a greedy sequence beside a sampled one), at 64 values per row from its first row. The drafter runs per sequence, eagerly, over its own ring; the lattice walk, the accept rule and the commit run per sequence (`commit(row0, n)`, and the drafter's context from the target features at `row0`). `speculate()` is the same steps through the same helpers (`plan_begin`, `plan_draft`, `plan_accept`, `plan_count`), with its launch sequence unchanged. What the build settled that sections 2 and 3 left open:

- **Who joins.** `Engine::can_batch`: speculation possible for the request's sampling and room for a full draft block. In `ling-serve`'s scheduler, when the request whose turn it is can join and another running one can too, they take one batched step (rows: the turn's request first); otherwise stage 2's unit runs (a prefill chunk, or the request's own one-row step). So two decoding requests step together every time, and a prefill still alternates with the other request's one-row steps (no prefill rows in the batch: that is the mixed batch). `kMaxStreamRows / draft_block` sequences fit one pass (2 at a block of 12 or 16). A batch of one is the one-row step, graphs and all.
- **A non-finite row** fails only its sequence (`SeqStep::error` holds `NonFiniteLogits`): its commit is skipped and its session reset (section 2); the other commits.
- **A lookup chain** (`--lookup 2`) replaces that sequence's drafted rows, so its row count differs; the concatenation takes any row counts.
- **RNG.** Each sequence reseeds its own generator (`p.seed + its history`) before its lattice walk and draws its accept from it, so a seeded sequence draws the same numbers in a batch as alone.
- **Counters.** `SpecStats::batched_steps`; a batched step's draft, verify and commit wall times are charged to each sequence in it, so `/spec_stats`' seconds per step read as a sequence's step period (`spec_report.py`'s step is then the mean over one-row and batched steps). `ling:batched_steps_total` and `ling:batch_rows` in `/metrics`; the request log line counts its batched passes.
- **Eager.** The batch path runs eagerly; with `--graphs` a request's steps outside a batch still replay its session's graphs. Graphs for the batch path, one launch per state kernel and the drafter over 2B rows are stage 4.
- **The checkpoint store's full rule (the owner's decision, 2026-10-12).** The least recently used entry gives way instead of the latest position, so a store full of other prompts' entries still saves a new prompt's checkpoints (`test_used_checkpoint_store_still_saves`: after a dozen low entries from unrelated chats, the identical resend of a 2,080-token prompt reuses 2,048 tokens; on main it reused nothing). An entry is used when it is saved and when a prefill resumes from it or extends its prefix. One refinement of the decision as worded ("so new checkpoints always get saved"), **confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-13**: a prefill never evicts an entry on its own prompt's prefix, so a prompt longer than the store's slots × 2,048 tokens keeps its earliest checkpoints (the ones other sessions share, the system prompt's) and skips its latest, as stage 1 did for one prompt; pure LRU would make a cold 30K-token prompt evict its own system-prompt checkpoint. Visible in `--prefix-check --sessions 2`: prompt 6 (A again, in a new slot) now resumes at 11,360 instead of 11,488, because C's checkpoints, more recent, took the place of A's 11,488 one; every state is still identical to a prefill from scratch.

### Stage 4 as built (2026-10-13)

The descriptor is `kernels::SeqBatch` (`src/core/kernels.cuh`): the number of sequences, each one's first row, and its position, either the host's or `pos_dev[i]` (device memory the engine writes before a graph replays); the per-sequence pointers travel beside it as `SeqPtrs<T>` (a small array per kernel). Both go by value as kernel parameters, so a launch captured into a graph keeps the row split and the pointers, and reads only the positions from device memory: section 2's device-side table in the form a graph needs, since a batch's sessions and their caches do not change between steps, only their positions. What the build settled:

- **The batched kernels** (one launch over every sequence, each row computed as its sequence's own launch computes it): `attn_prepare_seqs` (row m in sequence `seq_of(m)`, its caches, its position), `attention_rows_seqs` (the x blocks of each sequence over its own caches; one set of key ranges for all, the furthest sequence's, a range past a sequence's last key leaving an empty partial that the combine skips, the argument a captured graph already relied on), `gdn_conv_seqs` and `gdn_recurrent_seqs` (`blockIdx.y` the sequence; the verify reads each window and state and writes neither, so the stage-3 copy of the window is gone), and the drafter's `draft_qk_rope_seqs` and `draft_attention_seqs` (each sequence's block over its own ring). Each existing kernel's body became a device function that the one-sequence kernel and the batched one both call, so the one-row path computes exactly what it did.
- **The drafter over 2B rows** (`Engine::draft_batch_launch`): both blocks through the drafter's five layers in one pass (its NVFP4 and BF16 projections read once for 2B rows, the grouped convolution block-wise with `block = B`), then each block's drafted rows gathered by the final norm, the LM head, top 16 and the selector's projection over all 2(B − 1) rows, and the lattice per sequence from its own anchor (`ids_dev + i·B`). A batch with a lookup chain (`--lookup 2`) drafts per sequence as stage 3 did.
- **Graphs for the batch path.** Per order of sessions in the batch (`batch_graphs_`): one draft graph and one verify graph per pair of top-K values; per sequence, its session's commit graph keyed by its slot in the batch and its accepted count (slot 0's is the one-row step's own commit graph: the same rows, the same position slot). With two sessions that is at most 2 draft and 8 verify graphs, and 13 more commit graphs per session at a block of 12: bounded, captured on first use, as section 2 asked for the 8-hour run.

## 7. Questions for the owner, answered

All eight were answered by the owner on 2026-10-10; each answer follows its question. The next step is the build order of section 6.

1. **Sessions against batch rows.** Two resident sessions for two batch rows keeps the pool gauge honest and the harness at two tasks. Four resident sessions (18.6 GB) would keep a third and fourth thread warm, but the gauge would then read 262,144 and the harness would admit five tasks against a batch of two, so the gauge would need a rule (sessions × context capped at batch rows × context, or the harness's divisor changed). Which? **Decided (the owner, 2026-10-10): two resident sessions, one per batch row.** The pool gauge reads 131,072 and the harness admits 2.

3. **"At the harness's pace."** No spec section defines it. Does it mean a per-request timeout the harness enforces on the first request of a task (the 35K prefill), a bound on the other stream's step period during that prefill, or both? The contended chunk size in section 3 is set from the answer. **Decided (the owner, 2026-10-10): a bound on the other stream.** The large prefill is chunked so that the generating stream's step never stalls beyond a set bound, about twice its normal step; the prefill may take longer.
