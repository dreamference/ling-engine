# M0: measure before building

October 8, 2026 · measured on the development GB10 (`second-puffin`: DGX Spark, CUDA 13.0, driver 580.178), with production's SGLang image and flags. Every number here comes from that machine. The scripts are in [`bench/`](../bench/). Recorded sessions are replayed locally and never leave it. Only aggregates are reported.

## Summary

1. **The bus.** The bus delivers **262–264 GB/s** to a GPU read stream, **96–97% of the 273 GB/s peak**. Four SMs are enough to reach 98% of that; DRAM latency is ~400 ns. The spec assumed 245 GB/s; every target is restated against 262.
2. **Production is faster than the spec assumed.** On replayed agent sessions, single stream, it decodes **47 tokens/s** at a **105 ms step** and **4.95 accepted tokens per step**. Two runs agree within 0.5%.
   - The spec's ~30 tokens/s and ~160 ms describe **two concurrent streams**, the condition its SWE-bench logs were recorded under. Two streams replay at 33.9 tokens/s each and a 145 ms step.
3. **The 105 ms step has no "65 ms of overhead".** Nsight Systems splits it as follows; the GPU idles only ~1 ms per step:
   - 86 ms of target GEMMs at **204 GB/s average (78% of the bus)**;
   - 8.6 ms of drafter;
   - 7.5 ms of DeltaNet verify;
   - 6.1 ms of attention over a BF16 KV cache;
   - 3 ms of commit, KV append and sampling.
   The removable part is ~25 ms on one stream: the FP8 GEMMs at 62–83% of the bus, DeltaNet's intermediate states, BF16 KV and the drafter's second pass over the 248k head. With four streams, the per-sequence state traffic grows and the removable part grows with it.
4. **DFLASH drafts a chain, not a tree.** The 16 verify rows are the anchor plus 15 drafted tokens along one path, which DFlash2's candidate selector walks greedily through a 16×16 lattice per position.
5. **Tuned SGLang:** see section 6.
6. **Go for M1.** At 95% of the measured bus, the engine's step is ~83 ms with the FP8 projections kept and ~70 ms with them in NVFP4, against 105 ms today. With the hybrid lookup branch, decode is ~70–85 tokens/s single stream, against 47 today. The margin over tuned SGLang is smaller than the spec claimed against "30 tokens/s", but it is real, and it grows under batching.

## 1. Memory bus

Method:
- **Kernel:** `bench/membw/membw.cu`, a read-only stream over an 8 GiB `cudaMalloc` buffer (320× the 25.2 MB L2), 16-byte vector loads with the streaming hint (`__ldcs`).
- **Sweep:** unroll × block size × grid, best of 5; then sizes from 64 MB to 8 GB, and a sustained run with SM clock, power and temperature logged.
- **Machine state:** idle machine, GPU alone.

Nsight Compute has no `dram__*` counters on GB10. L2 counters stand in: a 2 GiB read showed 67,108,864 L2 sectors (exactly 2 GiB) and 0 hits, so every timed byte came from DRAM.

| Measurement | GB/s | % of 273 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read-only stream, best configuration (`__ldcs`, 4 × 16 B per thread, 32 blocks of 1024 per SM) | 262.8 | 96.3% |
| The same, sustained 15 s (min / median / max per second) | 259.2 / 261.1 / 261.8 | 95.6% |
| Simplest kernel (1 block of 256 threads per SM, no unroll) | 251.9 | 92.3% |
| Read sizes 64 MB … 8 GB (median of 9) | 260.0–263.7 | flat |
| Write-only stream | 194.8 | |
| Copy, kernel (read + write bytes) | 226.9 | |
| `cudaMemcpy` device to device (read + write bytes) | 155.1 | |
| `cudaMallocManaged`, preferred location GPU | 164.3 | 60% |
| `malloc`, CPU first touch (pageable access through the coherent fabric) | 167.4 | 61% |

**CPU traffic shares the pins.** GPU read stream while N CPU threads stream their own buffers (`cpustream.cpp`):

| CPU threads | GPU GB/s | CPU GB/s | Sum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 264.0 | — | 264 |
| 2 | 245.5 | 26.8 | 272 |
| 4 | 230.2 | 46.0 | 276 |
| 8 | 211.5 | 61.2 | 273 |
| 20 | 197.5 | 71.5 | 269 |

The CPU alone reads at 130 GB/s with 20 threads (155 GB/s copying). The bus is shared to the byte: the GPU loses exactly what the CPU takes. A host thread that streams memory during a step costs the step directly. The engine's host side must touch little memory per step, as spec 16.9 says.

**Latency and bytes in flight (spec 16.1, item 1).** `latency.cu`, a single-thread pointer chase over 128-byte lines:

| Buffer | Random lines | Lines shuffled within 64 KB windows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–8 MB (L2) | 141–143 ns | 141 ns |
| 64 MB–1 GB (DRAM) | 398–403 ns | 396–399 ns |

At 262 GB/s × 400 ns, a stream needs ~105 KB in flight across the chip, ~2.2 KB per SM.

**How many SMs saturate the bus (spec 16.1, item 2).** The same stream, confined to k SMs, one 1024-thread block each. This run had the SGLang server resident but idle, which put the 48-SM figure at 238 GB/s instead of 262:

| SMs | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 24 | 32 | 48 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GB/s | 235.6 | 241.4 | 241.2 | 240.6 | 240.3 | 239.5 | 238.2 |

**Four SMs reach the bus.** A weight stream needs only a few SMs' worth of outstanding loads, which leaves most of the chip free to overlap compute (the DeltaNet scan, attention, sampling) with the stream (spec 16.6–16.7). Wave quantization of weight-streaming GEMMs (spec 16.8) therefore matters little for bandwidth; what matters is that every SM that streams keeps enough bytes in flight.

**Clocks, power and temperature (spec 16.1, item 4).** Across each 10-minute replay below:
- the SM clock held 2,405 MHz (minimum 2,372);
- GPU power peaked at 82 W, with a median of 42–54 W;
- temperature peaked at 79 °C.

No throttling was seen at this load. The prefill-heavy case is not yet covered: it was not run as a separate 10-minute test.

## 3. The replay

Method: `bench/replay/replay.py`.
- **Request shape:** Mightling's agent (puffin) talks to the server through the Responses API. One real request, captured by a logging proxy, gave the wire format: `instructions`, the `input` items, 11 tools (21 with the code-index tools), `reasoning.effort = none`, streaming, and **no sampling parameters**. The server therefore applies the checkpoint's `generation_config`: **temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 20**. Production samples; it does not decode greedily.
- **Rebuilding requests:** each recorded rollout is rebuilt into the exact requests the agent sent. The rollout's `response_item`s are already wire-format input items, compaction is applied where a session compacted, and the tool set matches the run.
- **Fidelity:** the server's prompt token count equals the recorded `input_tokens` for every replayed request (median difference 0).
- **Session sets:**
  - *main set:* 12 sessions, 2 from each of the six `im-*` runs, seeded, with 12 consecutive requests from each; the first request of each window is cold, the other 132 warm;
  - *tuning set:* 6 of those sessions × 8 requests.
- **Generation:** output is capped at the recorded length, and the model generates naturally under production's sampling, since SGLang cannot force tokens. Replayed outputs reach 77% of the recorded tokens.
- **Where the timings come from:** the server, not the client. Prometheus counters give end-to-end time minus time to first token, plus verify steps. With `--log-requests`, per-request `meta_info` is also available.

The warm requests resemble the workload of spec section 2: 160 output tokens on average, at a median context of 23.7k tokens, with 390–480 uncached tokens.

## 5. Where production's step goes

Method:
- **Tracing:** Nsight Systems 2025.3.2, with `--cuda-graph-trace=node`, so every kernel inside SGLang's CUDA graphs is timed.
- **Attribution:** NVTX ranges added around each phase of the DFLASH step (`bench/profile/patch_dflash_nvtx.py`, bind-mounted over SGLang's worker), plus SGLang's own scheduler spans. Each kernel is attributed to the phase whose host range launched it, through its correlation id.
- **Window:** 460 steps of the main set's sessions, single stream, warm requests at ~24k context.

Nsight Systems 2026.3.2, the newest in the CUDA repository, records **no** kernels launched through `cuLaunchKernelEx` or CUDA graph nodes under driver 580: only runtime calls and plain `cudaLaunchKernel` kernels. 2025.3.2, the release that ships with CUDA 13.0, records them all.

Single stream, median step 110 ms under the tracer (105 ms untraced):

| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify: GEMMs | 86.3 | the target's 17.6 GB of weights, 16 rows |

| Verify: attention | 6.1 | FlashInfer paged prefill kernel over BF16 KV, 16 layers |

At 95% of the measured bus, the same bytes take 70.7 ms. **~16 ms of the step is GEMM inefficiency**, most of it in the narrow (N = 5120) out-projections and the FFN down-projection.

- GEMMs: 89.6 ms (weights are read once, so more rows cost almost nothing);
- DeltaNet: 24.7 ms;

- accept: 1.9 ms;

## 6. Tuned SGLang

Method:
- **The variant:** production's flags with one change, in a scratch container from the same image (`bench/sglang/serve.py`, `tune.sh`).
- **The measurement:** replayed single-stream on the tuning set (6 sessions × 8 requests), inside a quiet window.
- **Reference:** the production-flag reference on that set, in three runs (one at mem-fraction 0.5, two at 0.6), is **48.5 / 49.1 / 49.6 tokens/s, a 109.4 / 108.9 / 108.0 ms step, 5.31 / 5.35 / 5.36 accepted per step**. So the noise band on this set is about ±1.2% in tokens/s, and a variant counts only beyond ~2%.
- **Memory fraction:** from 12:30 the variants ran at `--mem-fraction-static 0.6`, because a second engine was resident on the machine. With the page cache, SGLang's KV arithmetic otherwise refused to start. The setting moves only the KV pool size, and the two reference runs at 0.6 match the run at 0.5.
- **Comparison rule:** draft-length variants are compared on tokens/s, not step time, because acceptance moves with them. A flag counts as a gain only beyond the noise of two reference runs.

| Variant | Exact? | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--enable-linear-replayssm-spec` (SGLang's version of the spec's DeltaNet replay) | yes | **Not available.** In 0.5.19 and 0.5.21 it requires a KDA (Kimi-linear) model with DFLASH ("requires a KDA (kimi_linear) model; got a non-KDA model"). ## 7. DFLASH: chain or tree

Read from the pinned image (SGLang 0.5.19):
- **The block:** `dflash_worker_v2.py` sets the block size to `--speculative-num-draft-tokens` (16) and logs a mismatch against the drafter's trained `block_size` of 8. The verify input is the anchor (the previous bonus token) plus 15 drafted tokens. The comment says: "TARGET_VERIFY uses standard causal masking; custom masks are unnecessary here."
- **The path:** DFlash2's `CandidateSelector` (`models/dflash.py`) takes the top-16 candidates per position from the target's LM head and scores the 16×16 transitions between adjacent positions. `sample_path` then walks **one** path: the argmax at each step for greedy requests, sampled for the others. Grammar constraints use `GrammarTree.from_linear_chain`.
- **The verify:** a chain of 16 rows, with up to 15 drafted tokens accepted plus one bonus token. The selector's alternatives are scored but never verified.

The tree the spec proposes (the selector's alternatives at the uncertain positions, plus a lookup branch) is therefore new relative to production. The acceptance it adds is measured in M2, against the histogram in section 4.

One further finding: SGLang already has the spec's DeltaNet replay as an option. `--enable-linear-replayssm-spec` stores each draft step's raw inputs and replays the accepted prefix into an FP32 checkpoint, for linear chains only. It is off in production; section 6 measures it.

Why production logs ~30 tokens/s where the replay measures 47:
- **Concurrency.** The SWE-bench rounds the spec measured ran two sessions at once. Two replayed streams decode at 34 tokens/s each.
- **Co-resident load.** Production's machine also runs SWE-bench containers, test suites, the code index and the web UI. Section 1 shows that 8–20 CPU threads streaming memory take 20–25% of the bus from the GPU.
- **Consequence for the engine.** Its single-stream number degrades the same way unless Mightling's admission counts memory bandwidth, not only memory (spec 16.9).

## 9. Go / no-go, lever by lever

1. **Step time at the bandwidth bound: GO, smaller than the spec claimed.**
   - **The gain:** 105 → ~84 ms with the FP8 projections kept (1.25×), → ~71 ms with them in NVFP4 (1.48×). The spec's 1.8–2.1× assumed a 160 ms step; production is already at 78% of the bus on its GEMMs.
   - **Where it comes from:** the out-projections (N = 5120) at 140 GB/s, FFN down at 208, the DeltaNet intermediate states, BF16 KV, and the drafter's second head pass.
   - **What M1 must beat**, measured at M = 16:
     - **Kernels that must be beaten:**
       - cuBLASLt already streams the FP8 in-projections at 218 GB/s;
       - CUTLASS streams the NVFP4 gate/up at 225 GB/s;
       - an engine kernel below ~249 GB/s gains little there.
     - **M1's first targets** are the narrow out-projections (14.4 ms → ~8) and FFN down (15.4 → ~13).
2. **Hybrid draft: GO to M2, unmeasured.**
   - **Draft shape:** production verifies a chain (section 7).
   - **Histogram:** 43% of steps accept 0–2 drafted tokens, and only 3.6% hit the ceiling. That is the profile a second, independent draft source helps.
   - **Status:** the ×1.2 is still a simulation. It is measured in M2.
3. **Short requests stay short: downgraded.**
   - **Today:** on an idle machine a warm request's server-side time to first token is 0.31 s median, not 0.68 s.
   - **The lever:** the remaining ~0.1–0.25 s is ~3–6% of a mean request. Keep FP4 prefill for lever 4; drop the separate ≤ 0.25 s project.
4. **New sessions start warm: unchanged.** First requests take 6–12 s at 1,700–2,000 tokens/s of prefill. This remains the interactive-latency lever.
5. **Several agents share one weight read: GO, the largest measured gap.**
   - **Production at 4 streams:** a 148 ms step. GEMMs 89.6 ms, barely more than one stream's. The per-sequence state traffic grows with the batch: DeltaNet 24.7 ms and attention 15.6 ms.
   - **Engine estimate:** 70.7 ms of weights, plus 4 × (2 DeltaNet + 3.1 FP8 attention), plus ~9 of drafter and the rest: **~100 ms for the same ~20 tokens**, ~200 tokens/s against ~135 (1.5×). The spec's ≥ 175 target holds.
6. **Agent-tuned drafter (stretch): unchanged.**

**Overall: GO for M1.**
- **Single stream:** the engine is meaningfully faster than production. 1.25–1.5× on the step alone, more with the lookup branch.
- **Four streams:** ~1.5×.
- **Tuned SGLang:** the margin is restated once its variants are measured (section 6). The variants measured so far do not touch the parts the engine removes. Its DeltaNet replay does not apply to this model, and FP8 KV saves at most ~3 ms of 105.
