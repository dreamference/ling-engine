# Research survey: what the last year of papers offers ling-engine

October 9, 2026 · reading only: no kernel was built and no GPU was used for this report. Every arXiv id below was fetched (arXiv API record, then the abstract page and full text). A paper's claims are quoted as the paper states them, on the paper's hardware. **Our estimates** are separate, marked as estimates, and computed against ling-engine's measured breakdowns:

- **Decode step:** 115 ms ([M1.md](M1.md) §5, 31K context). Target weight GEMMs 81.7 ms (17.6 GB at 215 GB/s; the bus gives 262), attention 11.5, drafter 10.4, small kernels 3.9, commit 3.4, GPU idle 3.4, DeltaNet recurrence and conv 2.1.
- **Acceptance:** 5.2 tokens per step at block 16. 43% of steps accept 0–2 drafts; 3.6% accept all 15.
- **Prefill:** 35K tokens in 16.5 s ([M2-prefill.md](M2-prefill.md) §5). Attention 30%, NVFP4 FFN 27%, FP8 projections 25%, FP32 intermediates ~14%, chunked DeltaNet 4%.

SPEC.md is unchanged; only measured wins go there.

## Summary

1. **The biggest slice is still the weight stream, and the literature only helps diagnose it.** 81.7 ms of the 115 ms step is weight GEMMs at 215 GB/s.
   - **The ceiling:** at 250 GB/s the slice would be 70 ms, so the gap is worth −7 to −11 ms per step (+6–10% decode).
   - **What the papers give:** no paper solves 16-row weight streaming on sm_121. Two give the method and an existence proof on GB10:
     - a per-GEMM diagnosis of wave quantization and DRAM throughput (2609.12923);
     - a weight-only decode kernel on DGX Spark at "96% of the bandwidth its weight traffic permits" (2609.26333, batch-1 GEMV).
   - **First step:** an access-pattern microbenchmark (item 1).
2. **Speculation has two cheap exact gains and one expensive one, all measured on DFlash2.**
   - **Block verification of the chain we already sample (cheap).** The one-paper evidence on DFlash2 at 16 nodes (2609.33652, RTX PRO 6000): +3.6% serving throughput at concurrency 1, by changing only the acceptance rule. Our estimate: +1–3% under sampling, nothing under greedy.
   - **Trees built from the selector's 16×16 transition scores (expensive).** Same paper: +7.4% throughput at a matched 16-node budget with sampled siblings and traversal verification. A deterministic tree gave −1.5% under sampling. CAST (2610.00321) measured 5.9–29.2% from tree shape alone at 16 packed tokens on Blackwell (plain DFlash, mostly greedy). Our estimate: +3–12%. The cost is a tree mask in attention and a tree-aware DeltaNet verify.
3. **The drafter can be made better on the user's own traces, and verify already produces the training signal.**
   - **Draft-OPD (2605.29343):** on-policy distillation from the rejected positions, +11.2% acceptance on average. Training on the first rejected token alone loses nothing (5.94 vs 5.95).
   - **Carryover Drafting (2609.14717):** feeds verify's hidden states for rejected rows into the next draft, +7.9–14.4% speed on vLLM.
   - Together they are the recipe for SPEC §10's agent-tuned drafter. Our estimate: +5–10% decode each, partly overlapping; a training project.
4. **Attention has cheap wins in both paths.**
   - **Verify attention:** PAT's structure (2511.22333) gives the design for the verify-attention kernel being written now: all 96 query rows of a KV head in one CTA, a ≥12-way KV split, partials merged through L2 rather than DSMEM (2609.01864 measured sm_120's DSMEM stores ~10× slower than H100's). Estimate −3 ms at 31K.
   - **Prefill attention:** reorders measured on GB10 itself (2601.16032) and an exact conditional O-rescale (2610.02229 / FlashAttention-4's idea). Estimate −0.5 to −1.5 s of a 35K prefill.
5. **Settled by the literature, not worth building:**
   - drafting and verifying in parallel (a net loss here);
   - longer drafts (the 3.6% ceiling bin caps them);
   - FP4 attention (lossy, TMEM-dependent);
   - DSMEM cluster reductions on sm_12x;
   - lossy state compression;
   - re-proposing the copy (lookup) drafter. M1 measured +4% oracle; only AgSpec's "emission-form" indexing is outside what M1 tested, and it is a CPU-only check.
6. **Microbenchmarks for the top 10 total about 14 GPU-hours** on the development GB10, plus one overnight fine-tuning pilot for items 5–6. None is started: the GPU is busy.

The reading was split into five batches: tree verification, drafter acceptance, agent workloads and precision, attention kernels, DeltaNet and megakernels. For each paper we recorded the claim, the hardware and batch it was measured on, whether it depends on sm_100-only features (tcgen05, TMEM, 2-SM MMA, which GB10's sm_121 lacks), exactness, and 2–4 quoted numbers. The top-10 papers' key sections were re-read against the quotes before writing.

**Excluded from ranking.**

- **Papers already in SPEC §16.** About 60, among them Bole, TreeWY, LumoTree, Weaver, DDTree, DominoTree, xPress, D-Loop (2610.06011), DBloom, BV loss, DScale, MonoMoE, MPK, Ada-MK, Minima, MpFA, SonicSampler, Tail-Replay and SuffixReplay. They are cited where a new paper builds on them.
- **Work in progress on the `decode/m3` branch:** a CUDA graph per step, programmatic dependent launch, a verify-attention kernel. Papers on these are reported as input to that work or as "after m3".
- **What M1/M2 already measured and closed:**
  - the context lookup inside the current session (+4% oracle);
  - block 12;
  - split-K, tiles per warp and prefetch depth in decode GEMMs;
  - a single-producer warp-specialized NVFP4 prefill pipeline.

**Limits of this survey.**

- **Window coverage.** The API returns at most 100 results per query, newest first. Five broad queries filled their cap within the last 4–6 months, so earlier months are under-sampled except where the date-restricted speculative query compensates.
- **Paper count.** The pool of papers on block-drafter trees is unusually dense (about 30 between June and October 2026). We read the ones that measure on DFlash or DFlash2, or on Blackwell.
- **Generalization to this setup.** Almost every speculative result is on dense Qwen3-4B/8B at short context. Only two papers measure Qwen3.8-27B: Tsubame (2609.33652) and Disaggregated Quantization (2609.26333). None measures a 25–43K-token agent context.

## 3. The top 10 in depth

Each item names: the technique; where it lands in ling-engine (files and SPEC sections); what to measure first; and a microbenchmark plan with input shapes from this model, a success threshold and an estimated GPU time on the development GB10. The GPU times are estimates and include builds and warm-up. Nothing runs until the GPU is free.

### 1. Weight streaming at ≥245 GB/s for 16–48 rows

- **Papers:** 2609.12923 (method); 2609.26333 (existence proof on GB10); SPEC §16.1 and §16.8 already list the levers.
- **The gap:**
  - The verify's target GEMMs move 17.6 GB in 81.7 ms, 215 GB/s.
  - M1 measured the same 215 GB/s for a pure read with `stream_gemm`'s addressing, 232–235 GB/s for a contiguous read, and M0 measured 262 GB/s for a streaming read.
  - So the loss is the address pattern, not the arithmetic. Split-K, tiles per warp and prefetch depth measured nothing (M1 §5).
- **What the papers add:**
  - **2609.12923:** a per-GEMM accounting of wave quantization ("19.5% in decode" on H100) and achieved DRAM throughput, rather than one average.
  - **2609.26333:** a weight-only decode kernel on the same chip: "On its own, the LUT2 kernel reaches 96% of the bandwidth its weight traffic permits". That kernel is batch-1 GEMV with bit-planed weights, and the paper does not define the reference bandwidth. It shows that streaming near the bus on GB10 is reachable; it does not show it for 16 rows on tensor cores.
- **Where it lands:**
  - `src/core/stream_gemm.cu`: the kernel and the `retile_*` kernels, which define the tiled layout.
  - SPEC §9 and §16.8: "divide bytes, not tiles", scales beside values, evict-first hints.
  - The constraint is row invariance (M1 §3): the K-chunk order per output column stays fixed, so any layout change keeps greedy output bit-identical.
- **Measure first:** for each of the seven decode shapes, achieved GB/s, grid size mod 48 SMs, and L2 lookup-miss sectors (`lts__t_sectors_*_lookup_miss` × 32 B; GB10 has no `dram__*` counters, M0 §13). This tells whether the loss is spread evenly or concentrated in the narrow 5,120-output matrices (only 40 tiles of 128 for 48 SMs, SPEC §16.8).
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - **(a) Pure-read pattern sweep.** No arithmetic; one 1 GB buffer; L2 evict-first loads. Sweep the contiguous run per warp (512 B, 1, 2, 4, 8 KB), the outstanding loads per SM (8–64 KB in flight; M0 measured ~400 ns latency), and the SM-to-region mapping (each SM its own contiguous region vs interleaved by tile). Report GB/s against 262.
  - **(b) Per-shape table.** Run `stream_gemm` at M = 16 and M = 32 on the shapes
    - 5120 × 34,816 (NVFP4 gate+up);
    - 17,408 × 5120 (NVFP4 down);
    - 5120 × 16,384 (FP8 DeltaNet in, qkv+z);
    - 6144 × 5120 (FP8 out-projections);
    - 5120 × 14,336 (FP8 attention in);
    - 5120 × 248,320 (NVFP4 head).

    Record GB/s, time and grid geometry for each.
  - **(c)** If (a) finds a pattern ≥ 250 GB/s, prototype it as a retiled layout for the FFN gate+up shape only.
- **Success threshold:**
  - (a) finds a pattern ≥ 250 GB/s;
  - (c) reaches ≥ 245 GB/s on the FFN shape at M = 16 and passes `ling-stream-tests`' row-invariance `memcmp`.
  - That projects to −8 ms on the step.
- **GPU time:** ~3 h (a: 45 min, b: 45 min, c: 1.5 h).

### 2. Block verification of the sampled chain

- **Papers:**
  - 2602.16961, the proof that block verification is optimal among single-path verifiers, "5–8% faster" than token-wise rejection.
  - 2609.33652, measured on DFlash2 at B = 16 on an RTX PRO 6000: RS chain 114.7 tokens/s, block verification "118.9 (+3.6%)" at concurrency 1, "+3.9%" at 2.
  - On Qwen3.8-27B with DFlash2 (native depth 8), request-mean acceptance rose 0.03–0.11 tokens per dataset (e.g. HumanEval 4.26 → 4.37, Table 13).
- **Technique:** standard speculative sampling accepts token by token (min(1, p/q)) and resamples from the residual at the first rejection. Block verification decides the accepted length from the whole block's probability ratios and samples the correction from a block-level residual. The output distribution is provably the target's.
- **Where it lands:** `src/core/accept.cpp` (`accept_sampled`) only.
  - The chain is a genuine sample: `speculate.cpp` draws each position from the selector's 16-way distribution at the request's temperature, and keeps that q.
  - The target p is already the top-k-truncated chain that `target_dist` builds.
  - No kernel changes. Greedy requests are untouched, because block verification and argmax matching coincide at T = 0.
- **Measure first:** the expected accepted length under both rules on the same steps, computed offline from logged per-position p (top-20) and q (16-way). Both rules have closed-form expected acceptance given p and q along the chain. This removes the sampling noise that moves a replay run between 4.80 and 5.20 tokens per step (M2 §5).
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - **(a) CPU.** Add block verification to `ling-spec-tests`' chi-square suite: four cases, 300,000 decodes each, the same 99.9% bound.
  - **(b) Logging replay.** One replay of M0's main set (12 sessions × 12 requests, production sampling) that logs p and q per step. Compute both expectations offline.
  - **(c)** If (b) passes, two paired replays per rule.
- **Success threshold:** chi-square passes, and the offline expected tokens per step rise ≥ 2% (≥ +0.10 on 5.2).
- **GPU time:** ~1.5 h (b: 40 min; c: 50 min).

### 3. Trees from the selector's transition lattice

- **Papers:** 2610.00321 (CAST), 2609.22098 (TreeSpark), 2609.33652 (Tsubame), 2608.13524 (DARTree), 2610.11750 (TEV). The DeltaNet side is in SPEC §16.6: TreeWY 2608.20961, Bole 2608.01651.
- **What the papers say:**
  - **Tree shape alone helps (CAST).** On Blackwell at the same 16 packed tokens: "This shape alone makes CAST 5.9 to 29.2% faster than DFlash in all twelve Blackwell and A6000 cells" (plain DFlash, marginal scores).
  - **Use the transitions, not the marginals (TreeSpark).** On a drafter with a transition head, a tree built from marginals alone loses to the chain (2.60–2.72 vs 4.39 τ); it must be built from the transitions. Our selector scores 16 × 16 transitions per position, and the lattice is already copied to the host each step (`draft_propose`).
  - **Under sampling, sample the siblings (Tsubame, DFlash2, T = 1, 16 nodes).** A deterministic top-k tree was −1.5% against the sampled chain. Siblings drawn without replacement and checked with traversal verification gave +7.4%.
  - **Greedy has a bound (TEV).** For a deterministic tree, no exact verifier beats 1 + target coverage, and predraw-and-follow (target-sample matching) reaches it.
- **Technique:**
  - **Build.** A best-first heap over paths of the lattice, keyed by the product of `softmax(score[e][prev])` along the path. It is cheap on the host: ≤ 15 positions × 16 candidates.
  - **Width.** CAST's stopping rule: add a node while its path probability exceeds (current tokens per ms) × (ms one more verify row costs). Today that is 5.2 / 115 = 0.045 tokens per ms, so at ~0.15 ms per row (our guess, to be measured) nodes are worth adding down to a path probability of ~0.007.
  - **Greedy requests:** verify the deterministic tree with target-sample matching.
  - **Sampled requests:** draw siblings without replacement from the selector's tempered distribution, verify with traversal verification (Tsubame §4; exact) and keep a fixed shape (DARTree) so the captured graph stays static.
- **Where it lands:**
  - `speculate.cpp`: build, accept walk, commit of the accepted path.
  - `accept.cpp`: tree rules.
  - The attention kernel: an ancestor mask, which belongs in m3's verify-attention kernel from the start.
  - The DeltaNet verify in `kernels.cu`: each node's output must come from its ancestors' state.
  - **Commit is unchanged:** only the accepted path is replayed (SPEC §9).
- **DeltaNet tree verify, in two steps.**
  1. Path recompute. For each root-to-leaf path, run the existing recurrence from S₀ over that path's rows and write only the outputs of nodes not yet written. The extra work is (sum of path lengths − nodes) row-steps on a 2.1 ms slice: about +2–3 ms for a 16-node tree with ~4 leaves (our estimate).
  2. If that cost matters, the TreeWY closed form (one triangular solve with a tree mask inside the 32-token chunk form the prefill already has).

  The conv1d window follows parent pointers in both cases.
- **Measure first:**
  1. The verify cost curve: one pass at 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 chain rows at 31K context.
  2. The first-level headroom, for free from today's verify logits: at the first rejected position, the rank of the target's token among the selector's candidates given the accepted predecessor.
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - **(a) Cost curve:** `ling-run` verify passes, 31K context, rows {16, 24, 32, 48, 64}, 50 passes each (~30 min).
  - **(b) Shadow ranks during one tuning-set replay** (6 sessions × 8 requests, ~30 min): the share of rejections whose target token is the selector's 2nd–4th candidate.
  - **(c) Exact offline acceptance for trees of 16, 32 and 48 nodes, greedy, on the tuning set.** Each root-to-leaf path is verified as its own chain with the existing kernels: slow but needs no new kernel (~3 h).
- **Success threshold:** (c) shows ≥ +0.6 tokens per step at 16 nodes, or CAST's rule applied to (a)'s curve predicts ≥ +5% decode at its chosen width. Otherwise trees wait until the drafter work (items 5–6) has raised the chain.
- **GPU time:** ~4 h.
- **Our estimate:** +3–12% decode.

### 4. Verify attention structure, as input to m3

- **Papers:**
  - 2511.22333 (PAT): "All equivalent configurations achieve 92.3%–94.2% bandwidth utilization" on H100, from packing queries that share KV into one CTA and sizing the KV tile by KV length.
  - 2609.01864 (CREDIT): on sm_120, remote DSMEM stores at 2.14 B/cycle against 33.01 locally, cluster sync 404 cycles.
  - 2606.06521 (P-Cast): applies only if P·V moves to FP8.
- **Technique, for the kernel m3 is writing:**
  1. All 16 rows × 6 query heads (96 rows) of one KV head in one CTA, so the BF16 KV is read once per step.
  2. A KV split of ≥ 12 ways, so that 4 KV heads fill 48 SMs (at 31K, ~2.6K keys and ~2.7 MB per split).
  3. Partials merged through global memory that stays in L2 (~4.7 MB per layer), not through DSMEM clusters.
  4. The tree mask from item 3 as a per-row ancestor bitmask from day one.
- **Arithmetic:**
  - The BF16 KV at 31K is 16 layers × 31K × 4 heads × 256 × 2 × 2 B ≈ 2.0 GB per step: 7.8 ms at 262 GB/s against 11.5 ms today.
  - Production's 6.1 ms is at ~24K context (1.57 GB, ~258 GB/s).
  - FP8 KV (SPEC §16.10) would halve the bytes again, behind the needle check.
- **Microbenchmark plan:** a standalone kernel run at 31K context, 16 rows, head dim 256, GQA 6:1, random BF16 data (M1 found all-zero data reads 20% faster). Sweep split count {8, 12, 16, 24}, KV tile {64, 128} and pipeline stages {2, 3, 4}; repeat at 64 rows with a tree mask.
- **Success threshold:** ≥ 240 GB/s at 16 rows (≤ 8.5 ms per step at 31K), ≥ 230 GB/s at 64 rows.
- **GPU time:** ~1 h. This is advice for the m3 job, which owns the kernel.
- **Our estimate:** −3 ms per step (+2.6%).

### 5. On-policy distillation of DFlash2 on the user's own verify logs (Draft-OPD)

- **Paper:** 2605.29343.
  - On SWE-Lite with Qwen3-8B, DFlash runs at "592" tokens/s and Draft-OPD at "644 (9%↑)", acceptance 4.60 → 5.27.
  - Overall it "improves the acceptance length by 11.2%".
  - Training only on the first rejected token loses nothing: "First-rejected-only and Draft-OPD achieve nearly identical average acceptance lengths of 5.94 and 5.95".
  - AdaFlash (2607.19223) supports the same idea with online distillation (cross-domain τ 6.42 → 7.35).
- **Technique:**
  - **Data:** anchors from real speculative decoding, the drafter's own block, and the target's distribution at those positions.
  - **Loss:** forward KL on accepted positions; reverse KL with position decay on rejected ones.
- **Where it lands:** this is SPEC §10's agent-tuned drafter (stretch, after M4), with a concrete recipe.
  - **Logging:** ling-serve's verify already computes the target's top-20 per drafted row for top-k sampling. Logging (anchor position, drafted tokens, top-20 at the first rejected row) costs ~1 KB per step.
  - **Training input:** training also needs the target's 5-layer features over the context, i.e. a target prefill per training sample at 2,100–2,600 tokens/s.
  - **Placement:** it runs overnight under Night Shift's admission (SPEC §10).
- **Measure first:** from one logged replay, how far the drafter's distribution at the first rejected position is from the target's (KL, and the target token's rank under the drafter). That bounds what distillation can fix.
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - (a) A logging replay of the main set (~40 min).
  - (b) An offline analysis on the CPU.
  - (c) A pilot fine-tune: one night, ~8 h, on ~2M tokens of logged sessions, evaluated on held-out sessions with item 17's offline estimator so sampling noise does not decide.
- **Success threshold:** held-out expected tokens per step +5% (+0.26).
- **GPU time:** ~1 h for (a), plus ~8 h for the (c) pilot.
- **Our estimate:** +5–10% decode after full training. Wall-clock training cost is unknown until the pilot: the paper used ~120 H200 GPU-hours for a 4B target, and the Spark is much slower.

### 6. Carryover: rejected rows' target states as drafter context

- **Papers:**
  - 2609.14717: "increasing average acceptance length by 6.5–14.7% and end-to-end vLLM speedup by 7.9–14.4%". Concurrency 1, Qwen3-4B with DFlash: 837 → 903 tokens/s.
  - Its ablation: conditioning on target hidden states (+10.0%) beats carrying the drafter's own tokens (−1.8%).
  - Alternatives from the same idea: ReTrace (2608.29748, +6.97% acceptance) and DFlow (2609.06498, from scratch).
- **Technique:** the verify computes the target's 5-layer hidden states for all 16 rows, including the rejected ones. Carryover projects the rejected rows through the drafter's existing `fc` and per-layer K/V projections, adds one learned embedding, and appends them as a temporary window to the drafter's context KV for the next draft only. It needs a short fine-tune (the paper: 3 epochs from the baseline checkpoint).
- **Where it lands:**
  - The commit step (`draft_materialize` in `speculate.cpp`), which already projects the accepted rows into the drafter's context KV, would also project the rejected ones into a scratch window.
  - `draft_attention` reads that window.
  - Cost per step: ≤ 15 extra rows through weights the commit already streams, ~0.1–0.3 ms.
- **Measure first:** Carryover's own predictor, "re-convergence" (normalized re-convergence correlates with the gain at r = 0.96). That is how often tokens along the rejected suffix match what is later committed, and it is computable from a logged replay. The paper's DFlash measured 2.51 re-converged positions per rejected round.
- **Microbenchmark plan:** a logging replay (shared with item 5's) that records the rejected suffix and the next steps' committed tokens; CPU analysis.
- **Success threshold:** ≥ 2 re-converged positions per rejected round on our agent sessions. If that holds, the fine-tune joins item 5's pilot.
- **GPU time:** 0 h beyond item 5's replay; the fine-tune is shared.
- **Our estimate:** +7–9% decode after the fine-tune. Untested on a hybrid target at 25–43K context.

### 7. Prefill attention: L2-aware ordering and an exact conditional rescale

- **Papers:**
  - 2601.16032 (Sawtooth, on GB10):
    - L2 hit rate rises with active SMs because the 48 CTAs move in lockstep through the KV.
    - Reversing the KV scan direction on alternate query tiles cut L2 misses "from approximately 370 million to 120 million sectors … from ∼61 TFLOPS to ∼69 TFLOPS (∼13%)", and causal "∼41 TFLOPS to ∼66 TFLOPS (∼60%)".
  - 2610.02229 and FlashAttention-4 (2603.05451, not read in full): skip the running-max rescale of the O accumulator unless the max grows past a threshold.
- **Arithmetic:**
  - Our prefill attention runs at ~51 of 122 BF16 TFLOPS, with 255 registers per thread and one 6-warp block per SM (M2 §7).
  - One KV head at 35K is 35.8 MB, larger than the 24 MB L2.
  - Rescaling a 16 × 256 FP32 accumulator per 64-key tile is 128 FMULs per thread against 256 `mma.sync` per tile, a large share of issue slots in a kernel already at its register limit.
- **Exactness detail:**
  - The prefill path's rule (M2 §2) is that a token's result must not depend on how the prompt was chunked.
  - Reordering which 4,096-key range a CTA visits next leaves results unchanged: each range's partial is computed alone and combined in fixed order. So the Sawtooth reorder must act across ranges, not inside one.
  - Conditional rescaling changes rounding but is deterministic per token, provided the threshold test depends only on that token's own scores.
- **Where it lands:** the rows path's tensor-core attention kernel and its combine (`kernels.cu`), prefill slices of 256 queries.
- **Measure first:** L2 lookup-miss sectors of the attention kernel at 35K against the compulsory read (K and V once per query slice).
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - The kernel alone at 8K and 35K tokens, 256-query slices, with three variants:
    - (a) baseline counters;
    - (b) alternate range order by query-slice parity;
    - (c) rescale only when the max grows by > 8 (log₂ units).
  - Check `ling-prefill-tests` and `--prefix-check` bit-identity for (b), and agreement against the double-precision reference for (c).
- **Success threshold:** ≥ 10% kernel time at 35K (−0.5 s of 16.5 s), with `--prefix-check` still bit-identical.
- **GPU time:** ~1.5 h.
- **Our estimate:** −0.5 to −1.5 s on a 35K prefill. This is the first request of a new session; it is not a decode lever.

### 8. The DeltaNet verify recurrence and commit

- **Papers:**
  - 2607.16831. The B200 contest's best GDN decode kernel used "a one-warp BV=8 kernel … vectorized state and q/k/v loads, paired butterfly warp reductions, fused decay and delta-rule logic", and found "shared-memory staging hurt". It "was instruction-latency-limited rather than HBM-bandwidth-limited".
  - 2608.15533 (DeltaLog): an append-only state log with periodic merges.
- **Arithmetic:**
  - The verify reads 151 MB of FP32 state per step (0.58 ms at 262 GB/s); the recurrence and conv take 2.1 ms. So it is latency-bound, like the contest kernels.
  - The commit (3.4 ms with the drafter's KV) reads the snapshot and writes the state again (302 MB). Appending the accepted rows' (k, δ) and folding them in at the next verify's read would save ~0.6 ms of traffic.
  - DeltaLog itself shows its gains only at batch ≥ 64.
- **Where it lands:** the warp recurrence kernel in `kernels.cu`, and `commit` in `speculate.cpp`. The commit replays through the same kernel, so any change keeps verify and commit bit-identical (M1 §3).
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - The verify recurrence alone: 16 rows, 48 layers × 48 value heads × 128 × 128 FP32 state, random inputs.
  - Variants: one warp per 8 value columns, the gate pre-scale identity, no shared-memory staging.
  - `--spec-check` for bit-identity.
- **Success threshold:** ≤ 1.2 ms (from 2.1).
- **GPU time:** ~1 h.
- **Our estimate:** −0.5 to −1 ms per step; the append-only commit adds at most −0.5 ms.

### 9. One persistent step with per-edge counters, after m3

- **Paper:** 2609.12379 (ForgeMegakernel) on H100:
  - against SGLang with CUDA graphs: "1.03× to 1.28× with a geometric mean of 1.11×";
  - "The megakernel launches once per step and costs only 9.3 μs";
  - the waiting it leaves: "the megakernel 227–313 μs, of which 218–304 μs is spent at dependency counters".
- **What it adds to SPEC §16.7:** the synchronization recipe — a semaphore counter per producer-to-consumer edge and no grid-wide barriers, with a shared-memory buffer pool so the next instruction's loads overlap the current one's stores.
- **Where it lands:** after m3's graph and programmatic dependent launch are measured; the graph stays as the debug path (SPEC §8).
- **Measure first:** what m3 leaves, i.e. GPU idle per step and the ramp-down/ramp-up at kernel boundaries in an Nsight trace of the captured graph with PDL.
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - (a) Launch cost per node inside a captured graph on sm_121: back-to-back empty kernels, with and without PDL.
  - (b) A two-kernel toy — FFN up then down at M = 16 — as two graph nodes against one persistent kernel with per-tile counters. Measure GB/s across the boundary.
- **Success threshold:** the toy's persistent form streams ≥ 5% more bytes per ms across the boundary than the PDL graph.
- **GPU time:** ~1 h.
- **Our estimate:** −1 to −3 ms after m3; unknown until m3 is measured.

### 10. Emission-form copy sources (an offline check only)

- **Paper:** 2610.01108 (AgSpec).
  - Code that the agent read as tool output is written back inside patches with diff prefixes, and often JSON-escaped. Indexing the "emission form" of each workspace file "help[s] most in the first turn, raising the coder's AL by 13–39%".
  - Its baseline was an EAGLE-3 chain of depth 3, never a block drafter.
- **Why it is in the top 10 despite M1:** M1 found that copying from the current context loses to DFlash2 in every match-length bucket, with a best-of-both oracle of +4%. M1's matcher copies raw tokens, so text that changes tokenization when re-emitted as a diff line or an escaped string was never matchable. That one case is untested.
- **Where it lands:** `src/core/lookup.cpp`'s index (a second view of tool-output spans), and nothing else unless the check passes.
- **Microbenchmark plan:**
  - CPU only, over the recorded sessions (`bench/lookup_sim.py` and M1's shadow logs).
  - Add `+`/`-`/space-prefixed and JSON-escaped re-tokenizations of each tool-output span to the matcher's corpus.
  - Re-score the best-of-both oracle against DFlash2's per-step acceptance from M1's shadow data. If those logs were not kept, one shadow replay is needed (~40 min).
- **Success threshold:** the oracle rises from +4% to ≥ +8%. Below that, the lookup branch stays off for good.
- **GPU time:** 0–0.7 h.
- **Our estimate:** ≤ +4% decode, likely less.

## 4. What not to pursue, and why

| Idea | Paper(s) | Why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drafting in parallel with verify on a second stream | 2609.27396 | Acceptance falls 5.36 → 4.43 in the paper. Hiding our 10.4 ms drafter saves ≤ 9% of the step, and the verify already uses the bus; net about −10%. |
| Longer or looped drafts, dynamic draft length | 2610.07659, 2608.08721 | Only 3.6% of our steps accept all 15 drafts, which caps any extension at ~+5% and realistically +1–2%. Truncation saves nothing at batch 1, where verify rows are nearly free. |
| Deterministic top-k trees under sampling | 2609.33652, 2609.22098 | −1.5% on DFlash2 at T = 1 (Tsubame); rejection sampling over deterministic top-k siblings is biased (TV 0.46, TreeSpark). Sample the siblings, or use the tree only for greedy. |
| i.i.d. multi-path verification | 2602.16961 | Negative on Qwen in the paper itself (4.32 → 3.43 tokens per call from K = 1 to 4). |
| More copy drafters from the current context (n-gram, hidden-state keys, copy gates) | 2608.03839, 2609.20186 | Bounded by M1's measured +4% oracle; all were tested only against EAGLE-3 or weaker. Only item 10's emission form is untested. |
| FP4 attention | 2609.04105 | TMEM-dependent schedule, and cosine 0.94 against BF16: far too lossy for a target whose distribution exact rejection sampling relies on. |
| Cluster/DSMEM fusion and reductions | 2604.23553, 2609.01864 | sm_120 DSMEM stores are ~10× slower than H100's; ClusterFusion++ reaches ~64% of bandwidth with nondeterministic atomics. |
| Lossy state compression or approximate inverses | 2608.30386, 2606.06034 | Not exact. Checkpoint memory (1.26 GB) is not a constraint; the inverse is a fraction of a 4% prefill slice. |
| BF16 decode weights | 2605.20315 | Reads ~4× the FFN bytes on a bandwidth-bound step. |
| A new drafter trained from scratch (H-Spec, DFlow, LiLiCorr, DRelay) | 2609.24197, 2609.06498, 2608.20530, 2610.01439 | Each replaces DFlash2 or its selector and needs full training. Carryover and Draft-OPD reach similar gains from the existing checkpoint. |
| CUDA graphs captured per length | 2604.23467 | SPEC §8 already reads lengths from device memory, so one graph serves every context. |
| B200-specific microarchitecture numbers | 2512.02189 | tcgen05/TMEM only. The one check worth 20 lines: FP16- against FP32-accumulate `mma.sync` throughput on sm_121. |
